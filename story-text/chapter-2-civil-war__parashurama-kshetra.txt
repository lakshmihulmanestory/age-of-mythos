Kochi. The first Tuesday of April. Five-forty a.m. Parashurama is at the kalari's northern entrance reading the morning's Malayala Manorama. He has read the Manorama every dawn for thirty-three years; the paper is delivered to the kalari's northern gatepost by an Idukki cycle-courier whose father had delivered it to Parashurama's guru and whose grandfather had delivered it to his guru's guru. The reading is a Malayalam-meditation; Parashurama does not speak during it; the kalari staff have been instructed for three decades not to interrupt.

This morning he interrupts himself.

He reads aloud, in plain Malayalam, in the slow administrative-elder voice he uses for student-discipline reviews: "Page eleven. Display advertisement. Three column-inches. Sea-Front Kalari, Kochi waterfront amphitheatre. Tuition Rs. 4,000 per month. Phased instruction in the One-Hundred-And-Ninth Form of Kalaripayattu. All qualified applicants admitted. Senior instructor: Sri Kala-Hasta Menon, formal kalari-trained at the Kollam Coiled-Cobra Lineage from 2008 to 2017. Daily classes 5:30 a.m. and 5:30 p.m. Inaugural class Monday morning the eighth of April."

He sets the paper down on the gatepost. He does not pick up his tea. He walks back through the kalari's inner courtyard to the senior-instructor's residence. He has not interrupted the morning paper-reading in thirty-three years. The kalari staff who see him walking back at five-forty-eight a.m. do not need to be told the routine has broken.

He calls the kingdom-council's religious-and-cultural-practice registry at six a.m. The registry's officer-on-duty (a forty-two-year-old administrative officer named Indira Pillai) confirms that the Sea-Front Kalari's registration was filed on the twenty-third of March under the standard kalari-charter; the application listed the school's curriculum as "advanced kalaripayattu, all forms including unsealed teaching"; the registry's standard review-period is thirty days; no objection was filed by any registered kalari during the public-comment window; the registration was approved on the twenty-second of April; the school's formal opening is the inaugural class Monday morning at five-thirty.

Parashurama says, in plain Malayalam, in the same slow voice: "Madam Pillai. The kalari-charter has admitted a 109-form school. The 109-form is sealed. The sealing is the kingdom's tradition. The kingdom's tradition has been amended by your registry's thirty-day silence. The amendment will require a kingdom-council review. I will be at the council chambers at eleven this morning. Please notify the chief secretary that I am coming."

He hangs up. He sits at the residence's desk. He looks at his own right hand. The hand has been holding the axe Parashu for forty years. The hand is fifty-nine years old. The skin on the back of the hand has the standard old-axe-bearer's calluses-and-thickened-knuckles configuration; the veins are prominent; the joints are slightly enlarged at the second knuckle of the index finger. The hand is not the hand of a man who can perform the 109th form. The 109th form requires the body of a man under thirty-five.

He has known this for the past nine months. He has not said it aloud. He does not say it aloud now. He picks up his tea.

Kochi waterfront amphitheatre. Monday morning the eighth of April. Five-thirty a.m.

The amphitheatre is a converted municipal open-air auditorium — capacity twelve hundred, normally used for monsoon-season cultural-festival performances. It has a single concrete stage at the western end, raked seating around three sides, and a sea-facing eastern wall that opens onto the Vembanad Lake's western shore. The acoustic-and-visual-line-of-sight properties make it well-suited to physical-performance instruction for groups up to four hundred.

Four hundred and twenty-three students are present this morning. They have paid the Rs. 4,000 monthly tuition in advance. They are arrayed across the raked seating in rows of seventeen; the seating-allocation has been administered by an entry-protocol that requires each student to demonstrate, at the door, a 108-form Stage-Five sequence as proof of qualification. Twenty-eight applicants were turned away at the door this morning for insufficient 108-form competence. The Sea-Front Kalari does not admit absolute beginners; the 109th form is built on the 108-form base.

Kala-Hasta is on the western stage. He is forty-seven. He is in plain Kollam-Coiled-Cobra dark-blue cotton, knee-length, tied with the standard kalari-instructor's leather-cord at the waist. He carries no weapon. He has the urumi (the flexible-blade-whip-sword) coiled at his left wrist as a wrist-bangle; the urumi has been the Kollam-Coiled-Cobra cell's standard side-implement since 1812. He was Maya-Sura's star pupil in Chapter One — the senior coiled-cobra disciple of the Naga-Padma cell who had escaped the Ch 1 climax confrontation with the urumi-and-cobra-venom training intact. He has spent the intervening years not in hiding but in the Bangalore-and-Gulf kalari-diaspora, teaching the 108 forms while privately practising the 109th. He returned to Kerala in spring 2025. He registered the Sea-Front Kalari on the twenty-third of March.

He addresses the four hundred and twenty-three at five-thirty-two a.m. He speaks in plain Malayalam.

He says: "Welcome. I am Kala-Hasta Menon. I will teach the One-Hundred-And-Ninth Form of Kalaripayattu in seven phased classes across the next twenty-four weeks. The phasing is the difference between the form's safety and its lethality. Each phase will be taught for three weeks; each phase must be completed before the next phase begins; no student will advance without my personal confirmation of phase-completion. The seven phases are: opening four movements; opening seven; first transition; full opening sequence; closing transitions; integration; private mastery. The first phase begins this morning. Stand. Find your row-position. We will begin with the standard kalari-breath. Breathe."

The four hundred and twenty-three stand. They take the standard kalari-breath. The class begins.

The session lasts ninety minutes. By six-fifty-eight a.m. the four hundred and twenty-three have completed the opening four movements' first introduction. The movements are slow, deliberately controlled, structured to allow the central nervous system to register the body-positions without the cardiovascular-stress that would activate the form's lethal mechanisms. The students leave at seven a.m. tired but uninjured. The session has been the safest opening-class of any Kalaripayattu instruction recorded in the kingdom's formal kalari-register.

Kala-Hasta stays at the amphitheatre for two hours after the class. He reviews the student-roster. He has identified, from the entry-protocol's 108-form demonstration, the twenty-three students whose 108-form mastery is at the level the seventh phase will require. He will personally tutor those twenty-three in the integration-and-private-mastery phases over the final six weeks of the curriculum. The other four hundred will complete phases one through five and will be invited to re-enrol in the autumn cohort for phases six and seven if they wish.

He has, in other words, built a teaching system whose pedagogical thoughtfulness is the chapter's most uncomfortable fact for Parashurama's tradition. The 109-form is not being taught as an act of defiance. It is being taught as a discipline.

Friday. The second week of April. Four-fifty-five p.m. The kalari's northern gate.

Achyutan-the-Nephew is at the gate. He is thirty-seven. He is Parashurama's elder sister's son — Bloodline-A by his mother's line, the same Bloodline-A branch that supplied Hampi-of-the-Bronze and Krishnaveni of AP and (through their long-shared maternal-line ancestor) Krishnadevaraya-Bhrata of Karnataka. He has been at Parashurama's kalari since age twelve. He mastered all 108 forms by age thirty-three — the second-fastest in the kalari's recorded history, second only to Parashurama himself. He is the kalari's senior daily-teaching deputy. He has, this morning, taught the eight-a.m. junior-students' opening-sequence class. He has, this afternoon, completed the final inventory-review for the kalari's monsoon-week ration-allocation.

He is carrying a single canvas bag. The bag contains: three changes of kalari-instructor cotton, his shaving kit, a notebook, a copy of the Sea-Front Kalari's student-enrolment-form filled in under his birth name (Achyutan Menon, no kalari-title), and the original 1907 family-photograph of his mother and her brother Parashurama at ages twelve and fourteen at Idukki.

He sets the bag down. He walks across the inner courtyard to the senior-instructor's residence. He knocks on the door at five-oh-six p.m.

Parashurama opens the door. He sees the bag's canvas-strap on the courtyard. He understands.

He says, in plain Malayalam, in the slow administrative-elder voice: "Nephew. Sit. The tea is the third steeping. The third steeping is honest. Will you sit."

Achyutan does not sit.

Parashurama is silent for about fifteen seconds.

He says, finally: "Nephew. The phasing. Kala-Hasta's phasing. You will follow it."

Achyutan says: "The phasing is twenty-four weeks. I do not have twenty-four weeks. I am thirty-seven; the form's upper-age window closes at forty-two. I will compress the phasing. I will complete the opening seven in the public class's first three weeks but I will practice the first transition privately on my own in parallel. The compression is my risk. I have read the form's mortality-statistics from Kollam — the open-window-in-the-second-month vascular-rupture risk is approximately four per cent for students who skip the phasing-discipline. I accept the four per cent. The four per cent is less than my certainty of losing the form-window entirely if I follow the twenty-four-week protocol."

Parashurama says: "Nephew. The four per cent is not a number. It is a name. The name will be yours. The name will be on the kingdom's death-certificate. The death-certificate will be filed by your mother. Your mother is my sister. Will you do this to her."

Achyutan says: "She has authorised the choice. I spoke with her last night. She said: If you do not learn the form, you will be a man who refused his own ceiling. If you do learn it and live, you will be the kalari's next generation. If you do learn it and die, you will be your mother's son who chose. I am the mother in either case. The choice is yours. She signed the consent-form for the Sea-Front Kalari's family-acknowledgement clause this morning. The clause has been part of the school's registration since the kingdom-council's amendment. The kingdom has accepted the risk-distribution. I have accepted the risk. The choice is mine."

Parashurama looks at his nephew for a long time. The fifty-nine-year-old's face does not change visibly. The thirty-seven-year-old's face also does not change.

Parashurama says: "I have not embraced you in eleven years. The embrace will be the only thing I have to give you tonight. The kalari's eastern gate is open until six. The Sea-Front Kalari's evening class is at five-thirty. You have twenty-four minutes. Take it."

He embraces his nephew. The embrace lasts about eleven seconds. He releases. He steps back. He says: "Go. If you live, you will return. If you die, your mother will return for the body. The cremation will be at the western grove regardless. Go."

Achyutan picks up the canvas bag. He walks to the eastern gate. He does not look back. He is at the Sea-Front Kalari's evening class at five-twenty-eight, two minutes before the call to position. He completes the opening-four-movements introduction class without injury at six-fifty p.m.

He compresses the phasing as he had warned. He completes the opening seven by the third class of his first week. He begins the first-transition's private practice in his rented room above the Mattanchery spice-warehouse the same evening. He works the seven-transition for six consecutive nights. The transition's safety-window requires the body to maintain a specific vertebral-curve through the central rotation; Achyutan's curve is slightly off — not enough to be visible during the slower opening-movements but enough to compound the basilar-artery stress through repeated full-speed attempts. He does not notice the compounding.

On the seventh morning — the second Sunday of the chapter, the morning of the public class's seventh-transition demonstration — he stands at his row-position at five-thirty-three a.m. He executes the seven-transition at full speed in the third sequence-cycle of the class. The vascular-rupture occurs in the basilar artery at the moment of the central rotation. He collapses at five-fifty-eight a.m. Kala-Hasta is at his side within nine seconds. The medical response is immediate; the school maintains a full-time-on-site emergency-response team per the kingdom-council's kalari-charter; the response time meets every protocol. The vascular-rupture is not survivable. Achyutan is pronounced dead at six-oh-six a.m. by the on-site emergency-medic, with cross-confirmation by the Kochi Medical College's standing emergency-physician via voice-call at six-twelve.

The cause of death recorded on the kingdom's death-certificate: Basilar artery vascular rupture during phased Kalaripayattu instruction (109th-form, phase-three uncompleted; private compression of phasing-protocol; cross-confirmed at Kochi Medical College). The certificate is filed by his mother at the Idukki District Civil Registry that same afternoon.

Sivakami-Acharyini arrives at the Sea-Front Kalari at eleven a.m. She is twenty-nine; Parashurama's elder brother's daughter; the kalari's senior 108-form deputy after Achyutan and the kingdom's only female 108-form grandmaster-aspirant. The charter does not say the truth out loud. My uncle will sign it tonight. He will not say the truth out loud either. The kingdom does not require the truth aloud. The kingdom requires the charter signed."

The teaching proceeds without interruption. Across thirty-one days Krishnadevaraya-Bhrata learns the 109th form in seven phases under Parashurama's direct supervision. The teaching-method is the master-and-chosen-single-student transmission-protocol that the kalari-tradition's deepest lineage has preserved without invocation since 1957. The method is not the same as Kala-Hasta's public twenty-four-week curriculum; the compression to thirty-one days requires the master's continuous attention — Parashurama does not leave the kalari grounds during the thirty-one days, does not perform his standard daily-teaching duties (which Sivakami covers), does not attend the kingdom-council's weekly sessions (Sivakami covers those too, with the kingdom-council's formal acknowledgement of her acting-grandmaster status).

Across the thirty-one days Parashurama ages forward visibly.

The visible aging is not metaphorical. It is observable to the kalari-staff who see Parashurama at the dawn-tea each morning. By the end of the first week his hair (which had been at the salt-and-pepper-with-more-salt-than-pepper configuration on day one) is fully white at the temples. By the end of the second week his hair is fully white across the crown. By the end of the third week his hair is fully white and his beard (he had been clean-shaven for forty years; he stops shaving in the third week without explanation) grows out in a fully-white configuration. By the end of the fourth week his posture has shifted — the standard axe-bearer's vertical-spine has rounded slightly at the shoulders. By the thirty-first day he is, by the kalari-staff's estimation, approximately seventy-three years old — the seventh, eighth, and ninth years of teaching have arrived in the four-and-a-half weeks of the thirty-one-day transmission.

Sivakami notes the aging-pattern in her grandmaster-aspirant journal. She writes, in plain Malayalam at the end of day twenty-three: "Uncle is paying the years. The transmission costs the master what the master would have spent across the next fourteen years; the cost is being paid forward in real-time. The bond is bargaining with time. The bond is winning the bargain. The form will be in Bhrata's spine on day thirty-one. Uncle will be seventy-three by the same evening. He will live the seventy-three-year-old's remaining life-arc — perhaps another fifteen years — instead of the fifty-nine-year-old's remaining arc. The exchange is honest. She is correct. Parashurama formally confers the 108-form grandmaster-title on her at the kalari's evening ceremony on day twenty-three. The ceremony is attended by the kalari's senior staff, the kingdom-council's religious-and-cultural-practice registry deputy (Indira Pillai, who had been the officer-on-duty at the chapter's opening telephone-call), and Kala-Hasta's formal observer (a senior Sea-Front Kalari instructor named Devika Nair who has been admitted at Sivakami's explicit invitation). The conferral is the kingdom's first female 108-form grandmaster ceremony in the kalari-tradition's recorded history.

Sivakami's acceptance-statement at the ceremony is brief. She says, in plain Malayalam: "I accept the grandmaster-title. I accept the daily-teaching seat. I accept the institutional responsibility. The 108 forms are mine to teach. The 109 form is not mine to teach — the inheritance-transmission protocol designates Parashurama VII as the sole 109-form master across this generation, and the form's next transmission will be to a single chosen Karnataka student named Krishnadevaraya-Bhrata. I accept the kingdom's charter-amendment that recognises Sri Kala-Hasta Menon's Sea-Front Kalari as the form's public-teaching site and the cross-school enrolment-protocol that protects students who study at both. The kalari-tradition is no longer one tradition; the kalari-tradition is now three. The three are: the 108-form tradition I now teach; the 109-form public-tradition Sri Kala-Hasta teaches; the inheritance-transmission protocol my uncle will perform once and once only over the next eight days. The three traditions are not in opposition. The three traditions are the kalari's honest answer to its own history. The kingdom is wider than any one of us. I will hold the 108 seat. The kalari thanks the kingdom-council's registry, the Sea-Front Kalari's observer, and my uncle. Good evening, all."

The ceremony closes at seven-fifteen p.m. on day twenty-three. The 108-form daily-teaching seat is formally Sivakami's from that moment onward. Parashurama returns to the inner-kalari pit at five-thirty the next morning for day twenty-four of the transmission. Krishnadevaraya-Bhrata is at the south-west position. The teaching continues.

Day thirty. Two p.m. The kalari's deepest sublevel — the sealed-chamber corridor that runs beneath the inner-kalari pit at four metres below the kalari's ground level. The corridor has been sealed at its eastern end by chains inscribed in ancient Malayalam since Agastya Muni's original sealing-ritual in approximately the eighth century. The chains have been visible at the eastern end of the corridor for eight centuries; the kalari-tradition's standard novice-tour includes the sealed-chamber door as the corridor's terminus.

Parashurama and Krishnadevaraya-Bhrata walk down the corridor at two p.m. on the thirtieth day. Sivakami is at the corridor's western entrance; the unsealing requires the senior master and the chosen student in attendance; the grandmaster (Sivakami) is not part of the unsealing's formal protocol but her presence at the corridor's entrance is the kalari's institutional witness. She does not enter the corridor.

The chains are inscribed in ancient Malayalam. The inscription, when read aloud, is the sealing-mantra. The mantra has not been spoken aloud at the kalari since 1957. The senior master is the only person at the kalari who knows the mantra; the mantra is transmitted master-to-master without writing; Parashurama received it from his own master in 1986; he will transmit it to Krishnadevaraya-Bhrata after the chamber's use.

Parashurama places his right palm on the chains. He speaks the mantra in plain ancient-Malayalam. The mantra is forty-seven syllables long. The speaking takes about thirty seconds. The chains release; they do not fall; they retract into the wall on either side of the door, into a small recess that has been concealed by the corridor's eastern stonework for eight centuries. The door opens inward by approximately two centimetres of its own accord, the door-leaf's ancient teak having absorbed enough moisture from the corridor's humidity to swell against the door-frame across the eight centuries.

Parashurama pushes the door open with his right palm. The chamber is approximately three metres by three metres by three metres — a perfect cubical room hewn from the Idukki bedrock. The floor is the same packed red earth as the inner-kalari pit but a different shade of red — an older red, the red of the earth before the eight centuries of human-occupation oils. The ceiling is unfinished bedrock with a single small opening (about ten centimetres across) at the centre that vents to the surface above. The walls are smooth, oiled, with no markings.

Parashurama steps into the chamber. Krishnadevaraya-Bhrata follows.

Parashurama says, in plain Malayalam (Sivakami is at the corridor's western entrance forty metres away; the cross-language translation is no longer needed since Krishnadevaraya-Bhrata has been learning enough Malayalam over thirty days to follow the master's instruction-register): "Bhrata. The chamber is yours for the form's first private execution. The execution is at six tomorrow morning. You will stand at the chamber's south-west corner. You will breathe the kalari-breath. You will execute the form's opening four movements; opening seven; first transition; full opening sequence; closing transitions; integration; private mastery. The form's full execution takes about forty-three minutes at the master-pace. You will execute at the master-pace. I will be at the chamber's entrance. I will not enter. The execution is yours alone in this chamber. At the form's completion you will return to the entrance. You will sit. I will close the door. The chamber will be re-sealed at the corridor's entrance with new chains and a new mantra. The new mantra will be a Kannada-and-Malayalam interlinear — the kalari's first cross-language mantra. I have drafted it across the past four nights. The drafting has cost me one more year. I am seventy-four by the chamber's closing. The kingdom is whole."

Krishnadevaraya-Bhrata says: "Yes, Maharaj."

They leave the chamber. They return to the corridor's western entrance. Sivakami is waiting. The three of them walk back to the inner kalari. Krishnadevaraya-Bhrata sleeps in the visitor-room from nine p.m. to four-thirty a.m.

Day thirty-one. Six a.m. The unsealed chamber.

Krishnadevaraya-Bhrata stands at the chamber's south-west corner. He is in plain kalari-student cotton, dark blue. He is barefoot. He has eaten one small bowl of rice-and-tamarind broth at four-forty a.m. He has drunk one cup of water at five-thirty.

Parashurama is at the chamber's entrance. He stands at the threshold; his right hand is on the door-frame; his left hand holds the axe Parashu, blade-down. He does not enter the chamber.

Krishnadevaraya-Bhrata breathes the kalari-breath at six-oh-one a.m. He executes the form.

The form takes forty-three minutes at the master-pace. Sivakami times it from the corridor's western entrance with a kalari-tradition water-clock (the kalari's standing time-keeping implement; the digital chronograph is also at her side as a cross-reference, but the water-clock is the formal record). The execution proceeds without error. The opening four movements complete at six-oh-three. The opening seven at six-twelve. The first transition at six-eighteen. The full opening sequence at six-twenty-seven. The closing transitions at six-thirty-five. The integration at six-forty-one. The private mastery — the form's final sequence, fourteen movements of breath-and-balance that integrate every prior phase into a single body-discipline — completes at six-forty-four.

At the moment of the private-mastery's completion, Parashurama feels the elephant-bond pulse through his own torso. The Stage III stroke begins to form across the elephant-tattoo's right foreleg — the only blank foreleg of the four-foreleg full-body elephant; the other three forelegs are inked from his Ch 1 mastery. The new stroke appears as a single new ink-line drawn across the foreleg in approximately fourteen seconds. Parashurama does not show the moment on his face; he is at the chamber's entrance; his right hand is on the door-frame; his left hand holds the axe.

At the same moment Krishnadevaraya-Bhrata feels the form settle into his spine. The settling is not a tattoo; the form's mark is internal — the kingdom's second physical-form-as-mark after the Karnataka pilot's elephant-bond-transfer at Hampi. He stands at the chamber's south-west corner for about thirty seconds after the form's completion. He breathes the kalari-closing-breath. He walks to the chamber's entrance.

He sits at the entrance. Parashurama closes the door. The door swings shut on its eight-centuries-old hinges at six-forty-five.

Parashurama speaks the new sealing-mantra in plain Kannada-and-Malayalam interlinear. The mantra is seventy-three syllables long. The speaking takes about fifty seconds. The new chains — forged at the kalari's western forge across the past three days under Sivakami's supervision — emerge from the door-frame's newly-fitted recesses. They cross the door at the standard sealing-positions. They lock.

Parashurama turns to Krishnadevaraya-Bhrata. He says, in plain Malayalam-and-Kannada interlinear (the language he will speak from this point forward in any formal-instruction context): "Bhrata. The form is in your spine. You will not execute it again in Volume One. The execution is reserved for the moment the kingdom requires it. The kingdom may not require it. You may live the rest of your life without performing the form. The form will be in your spine regardless. The bond's mark is on my elephant's right foreleg. The mark is the same mark as the one in your spine. The two are the kingdom's second physical-form-as-mark after Hampi. The Sanctuary will recognise both at the Chapter Three convening. Return to Karnataka. The road is the Western Ghats' mountain pass for eleven days. The walk will be at the standard pace. Sivakami will be your kalari's standing kalari-elder from this day. The kalari will send three senior daily-teaching instructors to Karnataka in the autumn. The kingdom of Kerala's kalari-tradition is now Karnataka's southern foundation. The Sanctuary will federate the two into one southern bloc under your Karnataka Dominant seat. I will attend the Sanctuary as Kerala's formal representative. Sivakami will attend as the grandmaster. I will not perform any form at the Sanctuary. I am seventy-four. I will sit at the southern observer's bench. The kingdom will hear my voice in writing only from this day forward. Walk now. The morning is honest."

Krishnadevaraya-Bhrata bows. The bow is the Karnataka student-to-master bow at the full-depth he would have given at the start. He rises. He picks up the canvas bag at the visitor-room. He walks to the kalari's northern gate. Govindan-Mama bows. Krishnadevaraya-Bhrata returns the bow. He walks north toward the Western Ghats. He will be at the Karnataka western mountain pass by Sunday afternoon. He will be at the Vijayanagara reborn-temple chamber by the second Friday after the chapter's closing.

The kingdom-council's emergency-amended Kerala-Kalari Charter is ratified at the council's formal session on the evening of day thirty-one. The session is attended by Parashurama (seventy-four; his first kingdom-council attendance since the transmission began), Sivakami (the new grandmaster), the religious-and-cultural-practice registry chief (Indira Pillai), Kala-Hasta in person (his first kingdom-council attendance; he had been invited by Sivakami three days earlier under the charter's cross-school-recognition provisions; the council had unanimously approved his admission as observer-with-speaking-rights), and the twenty-three senior tribal-and-civic delegates.

The charter's ratified text is six pages. The first page recognises the Sea-Front Kalari as the kingdom's registered 109-form public-teaching school. The second page establishes the cross-school enrolment protocol (any student studying at both 108-form and 109-form schools concurrently must register with the kingdom's kalari-medical-clearance cell; phasing-compliance must be documented; mandatory day-zero phasing-discipline session). The third page recognises the inheritance-transmission protocol as the kalari-tradition's third standing-pedagogical-mode (alongside the 108-form public-school mode and the 109-form public-school mode); the inheritance-transmission is reserved for senior-master-to-single-chosen-student transmission and requires the kingdom-council's prior approval per instance. The fourth page sets out Sivakami's formal grandmaster-title and seat. The fifth page sets out the kalari-tradition's cross-kingdom federation arrangement with Karnataka under the upcoming Sanctuary convening; the inheritance-transmission of the 109th form to Krishnadevaraya-Bhrata is recorded as the kingdom-council's evidence of the federation's pedagogical legitimacy. The sixth page records the day-zero phasing-discipline session at the Sea-Front Kalari with Achyutan's name as the charter's permanent witness-record.

The ratification vote is twenty-three to zero with Parashurama's vote not cast (he abstains as the formally-stepping-back senior master); the abstention is not a dissent and is noted as such in the council record. Kala-Hasta's speaking-rights statement at the session is one paragraph. He says, in plain Malayalam: "The charter is honest. The kingdom is wider than my school and wider than the inherited tradition. The day-zero phasing-discipline session is the school's permanent commitment. Achyutan's name will be read at the opening of every day-zero session for as long as the school operates. The school will operate. The kingdom thanks the kingdom-council, the grandmaster Sivakami, and the senior master Parashurama. The form is the kingdom's; the kingdom does not belong to any single school."

The session closes at eight-oh-seven p.m.

Two weeks after the ratification, a Sarus-pigeon arrives at the kalari from Vijayanagara. The pigeon is the one Krishnadevaraya-Bhrata has been using for cross-region correspondence since his return. The letter is from Krishnadevaraya-Bhrata himself.

Parashurama reads the letter at the kalari's northern gate at dawn. He does not show it to anyone. He folds it. He places it in the kalari-tradition's standing-archive box at the senior-instructor's residence. The box will be opened at the kingdom-council's seven-year review.

He picks up his tea. The tea is the second steeping. The second is honest.

Day thirty-two. Sunrise. The western grove of the kalari.

A single coconut on a single coconut palm in the grove has turned black overnight. The blackness is uniform across the husk — not the patchy darkening of normal coconut-rot; not the brown of overripe; not the white-speckled mould of monsoon-spoilage. The colour is the matte black of charred wood. The husk is, however, not charred; the fibres are intact, the texture is the same coir-fibre texture as the other coconuts on the same palm; the only difference is the colour.

The grove-keeper (an Idukki-born senior groundsman named Krishnan-Nair, fifty-eight, the grove-keeper for twenty-four years) sees the coconut at six a.m. He has been at the grove every dawn for twenty-four years; he can identify each of the twenty-three coconut palms in the grove by trunk-shape and crown-fan-orientation. This palm is the seventh from the northern boundary; the coconut is in the palm's second-highest cluster, the eastern coconut of the cluster. He has seen this coconut yesterday at the same dawn-rotation. Yesterday it was the standard green.

He reports the observation to Sivakami at the inner kalari at six-thirty. Sivakami walks to the grove at seven. She climbs the palm (she trained as a Theyyam-style coconut-climber at age twelve; the climbing-discipline is part of the kalari's standard secondary-curriculum). She retrieves the coconut. She brings it down. She places it on the grove's small stone offering-platform.

She returns to the senior-instructor's residence at seven-fifteen. She tells Parashurama. Parashurama walks to the grove at seven-thirty. He looks at the coconut. He reads the sign through the elephant-bond.

He says, to Sivakami and to Krishnan-Nair who has accompanied him, in plain Malayalam: "The Maw has experimented. The coconut should rot. The coconut will not rot. The decomposition process has been suspended at the threshold of the inheritance-transmission. The Maw is testing whether a sealed inheritance can be made permanent against time. The chamber was unsealed on day thirty for the form's execution; the chamber was re-sealed on day thirty-one with the new mantra; the coconut is the Maw's reading of the re-sealing. The Maw is asking: can you preserve the inheritance the way I preserve this coconut? The kingdom's answer must be: the inheritance is preserved by transmission, not by sealing. The transmission is what we have done. The transmission is in Bhrata's spine and in my foreleg-stroke. The sealing is the chamber's standing protocol; the sealing is not the inheritance. The coconut will sit on the offering-platform. The coconut will not rot. The kingdom will watch the coconut for eight months. The kingdom will not eat the coconut. The kingdom will not destroy the coconut. The kingdom will name the coconut at the Sanctuary convening. The naming will be the kingdom's response to the Maw's question."

Sivakami says: "Uncle. Will you name it now."

Parashurama says: "Not yet. I will write to Krishnadevaraya-Bhrata. The naming will be a joint-signature with the Karnataka cousin. The cross-kingdom naming will be the kingdom of Kerala's standing protocol for all subsequent Maw signs — no Kerala-only naming, no Karnataka-only naming; the southern federation will name the signs together. The naming will arrive by the autumn. The coconut will keep until then."

He turns. He walks back to the kalari. Krishnan-Nair stays at the grove to cordon the offering-platform with a small saffron-and-white twine perimeter. Sivakami walks back to the senior-instructor's residence to draft the cross-kingdom Sarus-pigeon cascade-alert.

Sivakami drafts the cascade-alert at nine a.m. on day thirty-two. The alert is sent to all nine bonded-kingdom senior guardians by sunset. The alert reads:

Bonded-kingdom guardians. The Maw has experimented on Kerala's western-grove coconut palm. A single coconut has turned matte black overnight without rotting. The kingdom-council's wildlife-and-agricultural-research cells will monitor the coconut across the next eight months under the standard decomposition-monitoring protocols. The expected mid-monsoon-decay-threshold is twelve weeks; the coconut is expected to not reach it. The Maw has demonstrated the capacity to suspend the natural decomposition process at the threshold of an inheritance-transmission — the saga's first material-permanence experiment. Mechanism: the coconut is the inverse of Tripura's brooding pigeon — Tripura's pigeon performed the action whose object was removed; Kerala's coconut preserves the object whose action has stopped. The two signs are paired across the South-and-Northeast boundary. The Sanctuary in Chapter Three will need to address both. The coconut will not be eaten or destroyed. The coconut will be named by joint-signature with Karnataka at the autumn. The naming-protocol will become the southern federation's standing Maw-naming convention.

By the morning of day thirty-four every senior guardian in the saga has read the alert. Kanchenjunga at Rumtek replies with the single-line acknowledgement and offers the Beyul-Kshetra's standing-meditation framework for the coconut's eight-month watch. Hmangaihi at Aizawl sends the NE-pair note: "Mizoram's two-headed serow named me; Tripura's pigeon brooded the absent; Kerala's coconut preserves the unrotting. The Maw has now produced three pair-experiments across the regions. The Sanctuary will name a fourth. The naming will be where the kingdom's answer is." Krishnadevaraya-Bhrata at Vijayanagara sends the saga's first inter-kingdom cross-region acknowledgement to the Kerala sign: "Maharaj. The coconut is the chamber's mark. The chamber is re-sealed. The coconut will sit. The naming will be ours together at the autumn. I will travel to the kalari for the naming the morning the coconut's eight-month observation completes."

At chapter end the western grove has a small saffron-and-white twine perimeter around the offering-platform. The black coconut is at the platform's centre. The kingdom is watching. The kalari-tradition is three traditions, federated. Sivakami is the grandmaster. Parashurama is seventy-four. The axe Parashu is at the senior-instructor's residence's wall-stand; he does not carry it daily any more. The 109th form is in a Karnataka cousin's spine and on his own elephant's right foreleg.

The kingdom is whole.

The next sign comes within the week.
