Saga Pattern • This Chapter
Gangaputra survives Chapter Two and earns his Stage III mark — but he does not earn it in battle. He earns it by stepping off the throne. His fourteen-year-old son Yamunadatta is acknowledged publicly and crowned. Gangaputra becomes regent-protector. The Krishna-shadow is fulfilled in dharmic form: the one who could have ruled chooses to drive the chariot instead. Per the male-led restructure he holds this protector-regent role into Chapters Three and Four; Yamunadatta is the visible throne-figure of the North Dominant.

The Hero (Steps Back, Survives)

Gangaputra — The Regent Who Set Down the Crown

Thirty-eight. Bloodline-B throne-heir. Hides his son for fourteen years out of fear. Acknowledges him publicly at the Lucknow Imambara and abdicates in his favour. Earns the barasingha foreleg-mark by surrender, not by victory. Becomes Yamunadatta’s regent and protector into Volume I’s end.

The New Throne-Figure

Yamunadatta — The Fourteen-Year-Old King

Half-Bloodline-B (Gangaputra) and half-Bloodline-A (his mother Radhika, a Mathura-Vrindavan singer of Bloodline-A descent). Briefed before the assembly. Accepts the throne and explicitly accepts his father as regent. Earns his own Stage I mark when the Sarus Crane lands on his shoulder.

The Catalyst Villain

Adharma — The Uncle-Kingmaker

Gangaputra’s half-brother. Returns from Banaras not as attacker but as righteous kingmaker — locates Yamunadatta first, publicly declares the boy the elder Bloodline-B heir, frames Gangaputra as the hypocrite. The most strategically brilliant Chapter-Two catalyst. Loses the moment Gangaputra acknowledges the boy and abdicates in the same breath.

The Three-Way Faction Split

Uttar Pradesh is three cities arguing in three languages. None of the three has been wrong about everything. None has been right about everything either.

Faction One
Banaras Religious Order
The Kashi pundits and Bloodline-B old-law scholars. Argue Yamunadatta is the rightful elder heir. Have Mahaguru Drona-of-the-Plain quietly behind them — until Drona’s public testimony breaks the alliance. Led publicly by Adharma.
Faction Two
Lucknow Secular Bureaucracy
The Lucknow administrative class, the chief minister Vidura-Babu, the state-revenue establishment, most of the diplomatic corps. Want stability above all else. View legitimisation as catastrophic for governance. Gangaputra’s base — until the Imambara assembly redefines what stability means.
Faction Three
Mathura-Vrindavan Krishna-Cult
Radhika’s people. Arguing from dharma alone: the child must be acknowledged regardless of political cost. They are not asking for the throne — they are asking for the truth. Their support for the acknowledgement (not the throne-claim itself) is what gives Gangaputra the courage to step down rather than just legitimise.

The Void Maw Sign

The morning after the Imambara assembly, the Ganga at Varanasi runs clear. Not muddy. Glass-clear, for one full day, at every ghat from Assi to Manikarnika. The river that has carried fifteen hundred kilometres of Himalayan silt and Gangetic-plain alluvium for two hundred thousand years runs visible to the bottom from boat to bank. Pundits panic. Tourists film. Scientists scramble. Gangaputra reads what the sign means through the bond: the Maw has reached the river itself. The Ganga has forgotten her own silt-history for one day. He does not retaliate. A small shrine is built at Assi Ghat where the clear water was first seen. The water returns to muddy at sunset. The shrine remains.
