Odisha's Void Maw Sign • The 13th-Century Stone Wheels That Turned Backward for Thirteen Hours

Tuesday week seven 12:03 a.m. The Konark Sun Temple at Konark, 65 km south of Puri. The 13th-century-CE Narasimhadeva-I temple is built as a colossal stone chariot with 24 wheels, each 3 metres in diameter, each carved with spoke-and-axle precision; the wheels are also functional sundials. The wheels have not turned since the temple's 1255 CE consecration. On Tuesday week seven approximately four hours after Jagannathi's chariot completed the standing-Rath-Yatra, the seven southern-face wheels begin to turn counterclockwise at approximately one degree per minute. The standing-on-site night-watchman D. Mohanty (54) confirms at 12:06 a.m. via torch-light cross-reference against the standing-monitoring-tablet's comparison-position photographs. ASI standing-on-call senior officer Dr. K. Patnaik (58) confirms at 1:14 a.m. The turning continues for thirteen hours at one degree per minute (each wheel completes one full counterclockwise rotation across approximately 360 minutes). The seven wheels stop at their original standing-positions at 1:03 p.m. Tuesday. No mechanical mechanism — the stone is not on bearings; the axle is solid sandstone; wheel-and-axle are carved from a single block. The Maw has demonstrated the capacity to turn carved-stone wheels backward through no mechanical mechanism. Paired with Assam's Reversed Brahmaputra (the river that flowed east-to-west for one hour during the Mahavidya rite at Kamakhya) as the saga's fourth formally-recognised cross-region Maw-sign pair: both signs are reversals of standing-natural-direction.
