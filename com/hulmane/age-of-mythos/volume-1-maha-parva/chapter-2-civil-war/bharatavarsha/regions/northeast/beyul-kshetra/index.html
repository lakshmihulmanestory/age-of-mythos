The Hero (Survives, Stage III by Boundary)

Kanchenjunga — The Guardian Who Finally Said No

Forty-one. Senior Beyul-guardian at Rumtek Monastery. Has welcomed every refugee for nine years as Avalokitesvara’s teaching required. The Chintamani is stolen from the inner sanctum during a public refugee-blessing ceremony. Pema-Chöden identifies Krodha as the Refugee Father. Kanchenjunga walks to the south refugee camp and draws the first boundary of his guardianship.

The Sixteen-Year-Old Witness

Pema-Chöden — The Tibetan Girl Who Found the Jewel

Sixteen. Arrived alone from the Tibetan side three months earlier — her parents and elder brother killed in the same border crossing she survived. Came to Kanchenjunga’s notice as a quiet camp-dweller who refused to attend Refugee Father gatherings. Tells him on the dawn of day 47: “The Refugee Father is Krodha. I know because my brother went with him last week and came back wrong.” Retrieves the Chintamani from a south-camp prayer-wheel. First refugee to hold institutional office in the Beyul’s nine-century history.

The Catalyst Villain

Krodha — The Refugee Father

Returns from his Ch 1 exile as a refugee himself, organising the camps over two years. Beloved by the refugees. Articulate. The most strategically successful Chapter-Two infiltrator. Exiled by Kanchenjunga’s boundary across the Lachen pass with the followers who chose him. Survives. Per D10 he returns at end of Chapter Three as the saga’s Fourth Herald.

The Three-Way Faction Split

The Beyul-Kshetra has always answered the refugee question one way: all who suffer are welcome. Chapter Two splits the answer into three.

Faction One
The Open-Door • Compassion-Absolutist
Senior monastery monks who hold that the Beyul’s vocation is universal welcome. Avalokitesvara’s teaching admits no exceptions. The Chintamani theft does not justify a policy reversal. Kanchenjunga’s base for nine years — until he walks past them.
Faction Two
The Sealed-Door • Security-Absolutist
The junior monks plus the Sikkim state administration. Demand the Beyul close to all refugees pending identity-verification of every camp-dweller. Cite the Chintamani theft. Would also expel Pema-Chöden. Wrong about her; right that something must change.
Faction Three
The Refugees Themselves
Twelve thousand displaced persons across three south-Sikkim camps. The third faction in their own right — treated as the question for nine years; recognised as the answerers this chapter. Kanchenjunga’s boundary lets them choose: stay-and-refuse-Krodha, or leave-with-him. About fifty-fifty.

The Void Maw Sign

A red panda is sighted at five thousand four hundred metres altitude on the western face of Khangchendzonga — an elevation that should be physiologically lethal to the species (red pandas live at 2,200–4,800 m and cannot survive the oxygen and cold above 5,000). The panda is alive. It is grazing on the snowline edge as if the altitude is unremarkable. It will not descend. Park rangers observe it for nine consecutive days through high-power optics. It does not move below 5,400 m. The panda’s ear-tag (from a 2022 conservation tagging) confirms it as a known Khangchendzonga National Park individual previously recorded at 3,800 m. Kanchenjunga reads the sign: the Maw is testing whether bonded animals can be sent to dwell at elevations that should kill them. Krodha’s Herald-transformation is approaching.
