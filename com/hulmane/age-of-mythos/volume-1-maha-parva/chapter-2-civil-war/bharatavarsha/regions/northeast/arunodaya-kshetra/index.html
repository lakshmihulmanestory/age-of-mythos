Concession-Trial • This Chapter
Arunodaya-Kshetra survives Chapter Two and Udayagiri earns his Stage III gayal-foreleg mark not by defeating the Comforter, not by destroying the night-cult, not by refusing or boundary-drawing, but by publicly conceding that shade is needed. He builds the first Dawn-Shadow Temple at Tawang in the eleventh week of the chapter — a structure that holds both light and managed darkness in the same enclosed space. Asta-Asura survives, is integrated as the Temple’s shade-master, his followers retain their cult-name but become a recognised religious order with state-government registration. Tani-of-the-Veil, a former night-cultist, becomes Udayagiri’s translator between the two communities and the saga’s first chapter-two co-administrator who came across from a villain’s side. The First Herald appears in the dawn-tribe village at Roing on the morning the Temple opens. Per restructure §1 Arunachal joins the NE Federation under Moirangthem in Ch 3 after the Herald is named.

The Hero (Survives, Stage III by Concession)

Udayagiri — The Dawn-Walker Who Built the First Shadow

Thirty-one. The Dawn-Walker who has greeted the sun before anyone else in India for nine years. The Tawang night-cult forms among war-trauma survivors and bereaved families who cannot bear the morning light. The Comforter arrives offering shade and is loved for it. Udayagiri’s mentor Baba Parashar — the cloud-speaker Karna-shadow elder — refuses to choose a side and is killed by both in the same hour at the Tawang valley pass. Udayagiri spends eight days alone above the cloud-line, returns with the architectural plans for the first Dawn-Shadow Temple.

The Catalyst (Integrated, Not Defeated)

Asta-Asura — The Comforter

Forty-four. Returned from his Ch 1 disappearance as a soft-voiced welcomer of the unhealed; gives shade to the burned. Has built the Tawang night-cult to eleven hundred members in fourteen months by genuinely listening to grief. His followers love him. They are not wrong. Survives the chapter. Becomes the shade-master of the new Dawn-Shadow Temple at the eleventh-week consecration. The saga’s second integrated catalyst-villain (after Kama-Mara at Kamakhya). The argument he carries is correct; the methods are dangerous; the integration solves both.

The Karna-Shadow Loss

Baba Parashar — The Cloud-Speaker (Deceased Week 7)

Sixty-eight. Udayagiri’s mentor since age twelve. The hermit-prophet who lives above the cloud-line and converses with weather. Refuses to choose between dawn-faction and night-cult; calls the dispute “a sibling argument the universe is taking too seriously.” Killed at the Tawang valley pass in the same hour by both factions — two arrows, one from each side, fired without coordination. Cremated above the cloud-line by Udayagiri and Tani-of-the-Veil together — the first joint act across the faction-line.

The Three-Way Faction Split

The Land of the Rising Sun has always answered the trauma question one way: greet the dawn, walk the morning, the night will pass. Chapter Two splits the answer into three.

Faction One
The Dawn-Tribes • Light-Absolutist
The thirteen original sun-tribes who hold that the kingdom’s vocation is unbroken first-light. They will not accept any institutional shade. They are not wrong about the kingdom’s founding charter. They are wrong about who its trauma can carry. Udayagiri’s base for nine years — until he walks past them at the eighth dawn.
Faction Two
The Night-Cult • Shade-Absolutist
Eleven hundred members in fourteen months. Mostly war-trauma survivors from the 2020 Sino-Indian Galwan-aftermath, bereaved families of the 2022 Tawang clash dead, and burned survivors of the 2024 Ziro forest fires. Cannot bear the morning light. The Comforter’s shade is the first relief any of them have found. Their grievance is real. Their isolation is the kingdom’s failure, not their fault. About half a kilometre east of Tawang Monastery, in the Sela Pass shadow-zone.
Faction Three
The Veil • The Crossings
The chapter’s new third position. Initially Tani-of-the-Veil and three other night-cultists who cross over to Udayagiri’s side in week eight after Baba Parashar’s funeral. Grows by month’s end to about seventy crossings — half from each direction, dawn-tribers who recognised the shade-need and night-cultists who recognised the dawn-need. By the Dawn-Shadow Temple’s consecration the Veil is the recognised institutional bridge between the two factions and Tani is its registered chair.

The Void Maw Sign — The First Herald

The morning of the Dawn-Shadow Temple’s consecration. Roing village, lower Dibang valley. A man walks through the village at first light. He is dressed in a plain Adi-tribe homespun cotton shawl. He is barefoot. He carries nothing. He walks the length of the village’s main path from east to west, takes about eleven minutes, and continues out of the village toward the river. He has no shadow. The first-light sun is at the appropriate eastern angle to cast every person, animal, and post-mounted lamp a long westward shadow on the dirt path. The man casts no shadow at all. The dirt-path beneath his bare feet shows no shadow-track, no foot-impression, no compression. The witnesses report this observation independently; the absence is not in dispute. Within ninety minutes of his passage, none of the witnesses can remember what he looked like. They remember he was a man. They remember he was dressed in homespun. They remember he had no shadow. They cannot describe his face. They cannot draw his face. They cannot agree on whether he was tall or short, young or old, Adi or Galo or Apatani or non-tribal. The face has erased itself from their memory. The First Herald has walked the village. The kingdom now knows what it had only suspected: the Maw can take a man and remove his face from the world’s memory.
