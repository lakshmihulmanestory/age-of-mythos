What Changed Since Chapter One

In Chapter One Dzulevira defeated Naga-Rakshasa at the Dzukou Valley confrontation and drove him east into the Patkai Hills border-zone. He spent two years there in unaccounted wandering. During those two years he learned each of the sixteen Naga tribal dialects perfectly — a feat no living Naga had ever achieved — and forged a set of sixteen DNA-rite kits that “proved” each tribe’s ancestral primacy in mutually exclusive ways. He returned in autumn of Chapter Two’s year and began visiting villages. By month four every tribe was demanding purity-rites of its members. By month five Dzulevira’s elder sister Ka-Memcha — visiting Ukhrul from Imphal — was killed for refusing one.

The Pre-Tribal Pact — 600 Years Old, Hidden Under a Floor for 41

Khrieo-Mama’s father’s grandfather inherited the Pact from the Kohima elder-council in 1923. It had been hidden by the Angami matriarchs since 1882 when British colonial administration began assigning “tribe” as a fixed identity for census purposes. Khrieo-Mama herself has kept it under the floorboards of her ancestral house for 41 years. She brings it out in week four of Chapter Two when she sees what the Pure-Tongue is doing.

The Pact • Translated From Old-Angami
“Before the sixteen, there was one. Before the names, there was the mithun. The names are our work. The mithun is our root. Let no one of us claim purity above another. Let the mithun walk between us when the sixteen fight. The one who walks beside the mithun without a tribe is the one who holds the kingdom.”
— Signed by the founders of all sixteen Naga tribes at Kohima, on the third full moon of the year corresponding to 1425 CE in the Gregorian calendar. Sixteen signatures, each in the tribe’s own pre-modern script. The clay-tablet original is preserved at Khrieo-Mama’s house; a paper transcription is now lodged with the Nagaland State Archives and is published in seventeen languages (sixteen tribal dialects plus Hindi).

Key Characters — This Chapter

Hero • Survives, Stage III by Refusal

Dzulevira

The Walker Who Refused All Sixteen • Bloodline C • Draupadi-mapped

Thirty-three. Tangkhul-Naga by birth, raised in Ukhrul by her matriarch-grandmother. The second of three sisters — the eldest Ka-Memcha is killed in Tangkhul tribal purity-rite at week three; the youngest is Dzulevira; the middle-sister was killed at twenty-one in a 2017 landslide. Studied Naga linguistics at NEHU Shillong. Trained in mithun-bonding at the Dzukou Valley grass-fields since age seven. The state hero by political election in 2022 — the first elected Naga political voice in eleven years. Receives the news of her sister’s death at Kohima while she is mid-meeting with the Hornbill Festival cancellation committee. Goes silent for two days. Khrieo-Mama arrives on day three with the Pact. Begins the walk on day five. Walks for twenty-seven days. Earns Stage III at the sixteenth village (Pochury) by answering Naga-Rakshasa’s sixteen-dialect challenge in Hindi.

Senior Elder • Pact-Bringer

Khrieo-Mama

The 89-Year-Old Angami Matriarch

Eighty-nine. Eldest Angami matriarch of the Khonoma village near Kohima. Has kept the Pre-Tribal Pact under the floorboards of her ancestral house since 1985 when her own grandmother died. Was waiting for the right hour to bring it out. Sees the right hour in week four when the Tangkhul purity-rite kills Ka-Memcha. Walks the seven kilometres from Khonoma to Kohima alone with the clay tablet wrapped in her shawl. Arrives at Dzulevira’s house at midnight. Provides the sixteen translations of the Pact (she speaks seven of the dialects fluently, has translation-help from her granddaughters for the other nine) over the four days before the walk begins. Walks the first three valleys with Dzulevira before her knees give out; rides in the support-vehicle for the remaining thirteen. Becomes Dzulevira’s senior advisor through Volume I.

Family Loss • Killed in Tangkhul Purity-Rite

Ka-Memcha (deceased)

The Eldest Sister, Manipur-Married, Mother of Eight-Year-Old Sangaputra

Thirty-five. Eldest of the three Tangkhul-Naga sisters. Married Moirangthem of Manipur in 2019 (the Bloodline-C reinforcement marriage that became love-marriage). Mother of Sangaputra, the eight-year-old who discovered the pause at Loktak. Returns to Ukhrul in week two of Chapter Two to help her sister Dzulevira with the sixteen-tribe purity wars. The Tangkhul tribal elders demand she submit to a purity-rite because she is “married-out” to Meitei Manipur and her son is “half-impure.” She refuses publicly at the Tangkhul ceremony-grounds at Ukhrul on the third day of week three. She says, on record: “I am Tangkhul and I am Meitei. Both. My son is both. Neither is impure.” The next morning at first light she is pushed into a ritual fire by senior Tangkhul priest Khrumai-Sho. The killing is recorded by the priest as “ceremonial-accident.” Khrumai-Sho is arrested four hours later by Dzulevira’s state-police detail. Ka-Memcha’s body is cremated according to combined Tangkhul-Meitei rites by explicit prior wish of the family. Her ashes are returned to Imphal for installation at the Govindaji temple alongside the nine massacre-dancers from Loktak.

Catalyst Villain • Defeated, Exiled

Naga-Rakshasa • The Pure-Tongue

The Sixteen-Dialect Manipulator

Forty-six. Learned all sixteen Naga tribal dialects during his two-year post-Ch-1 wandering in the Patkai Hills border-zone. The feat has never been documented in any living Naga — the dialects diverged across centuries of separation and most are mutually unintelligible. Forged sixteen DNA-rite kits at a private lab in Chiang Mai (Thai-Burmese border facility, traced by the Mumbai-Mizoram weapons-channel to the same Singapore-funding line that supplied Megha-Daitya’s submachine guns and Tamasa-Nrita’s daos). Returns autumn 2026. Visits sixty-three villages across six months. Each tribe’s elders receive him as one of their own. Each tribe’s DNA-test “proves” them the original Naga line. Sixteen mutually exclusive claims. Defeated by Dzulevira’s Hindi-refusal at the Pochury confrontation. Exiled across the Burmese border. Per Chapter Three: he and Tamasa-Nrita are both Adharma-Kendra’s NE field-operatives by the time the Sanctuary convenes.

Cosmic Sign • The Python in the Loop

The Python That Could Not Let Go

Nagaland’s Void Maw Sign • First Self-Cancelling Form

Found on the path Dzulevira walked through Pochury village, at first light the morning after the confrontation. A Burmese python — common in the Patkai-Naga hills, average length two to three metres — has swallowed its own tail. The constriction has cut off its own breathing. It is dead in the loop. The body is intact. The constriction has not loosened in death. The tail remains in the mouth. Khrieo-Mama identifies the sign from the Naga tribal commentaries on the Kalika Purana’s footnotes — the “Uroboros-fail” pattern that pre-modern tantric texts described as the form of self-cancelling logic. The Pure-Tongue’s sixteen mutually exclusive purity-claims are the same form: each tribe’s purity argument requires every other tribe’s purity-impossibility, and the constriction has cut off the breathing of the whole. The python is preserved at the Pochury village in a small museum-shrine designed by Khrieo-Mama; the shrine becomes a pilgrimage site within the season.
