The Hero (Final Act)

Sagaradeva — The Tide-Keeper Who Honored the Old Promise

Forty-two. Karna-shadow. Made a promise to defend the Goan coast as a pure tide-keeper — refused to accept that Goa was never pure. The promise drove the choice. The choice drove the storm. The storm drove the death.

The Catalyst Villain

Maha-Vega — Now in Foreign Service

Sagaradeva’s storm-cursed half-brother. Returns allied with a multinational naval fleet. Tries to recruit Sagaradeva to the foreign side as “brothers protecting Goa together.” Killed by Sagaradeva in the bay moments before Sagaradeva himself falls.

The Inheritor of Resistance

Ferreira-de-Sangam — The Daughter of the Mixed Tide

Half-Portuguese (Pernem-line), half-Goan. Thirty-three. Walked into Sagaradeva’s hut three nights before the storm and offered to help. He refused. After his death, she begins the work he would not begin. The Goan resistance into Ch 3 is hers.

The Three-Way Faction Split

Goa fractures along the foreign tide. The fishermen want their coast back. The port consortium wants modernization money. The foreign fleet wants both.

Faction One • ~30%
The Old Fishermen
The Tendulkar-led coastal villages who have fished the Konkan since before Vijayanagara. Sagaradeva’s base. Lose their fishing rights when Mormugao port is leased to the consortium. Lose their patriarch Bhau Tendulkar to a corporate “accident.”
Faction Two • ~40%
The Port Consortium
A multinational corporation that has bought the Mormugao port lease and surrounding land — partly Indian, partly foreign-funded. Builds a deepwater facility designed to harbor military-grade vessels. Faces of dignity. Hands of erasure.
Faction Three • ~30%
The Foreign Heritage Fleet
A multinational naval force sailing into Mormugao under a “heritage exchange” pretext. Their flagship carries Maha-Vega in some unholy concord. They claim cultural continuity with seventeenth-century colonial powers; their cargo holds twenty-first-century missiles.

The Void Maw Sign

The Second Herald walks out of the surf at Old Goa at dawn the day after the storm. He wears the clothes of a seventeenth-century sailor. He is wet but no fishermen recognize him. He cannot remember his name, his ship, or the sea that brought him. Ferreira-de-Sangam takes him in. She gives him a name: Vasco-without-a-ship. The Karnataka rule holds: the Herald is named, not killed.
