What Changed Since Chapter One

Six months have passed since Tungabhadra defeated Ravana-Putra and dissolved the stone army through the musical pillars of Hampi. Ravana-Putra sat down. He ate rice. He wept and faded into the work of restoration. But the deeper villain — Bhoomi-Takshaka, the serpent-bonded Bloodline-A dispossessed-branch heir — was never killed. She was waiting. And while Tungabhadra was rebuilding, Bhoomi-Takshaka was reading the imperial archives. She found something. She found that the serpent-network beneath Hampi was real Vijayanagara security infrastructure, not a curse but a vault. She did not have to attack anymore. She could simply offer to restore.

The Three Factions of Karnataka

The kingdom no longer has one villain and one hero. It has three factions that cannot all be right. The civil war is the discovery that two of them must let go of something before the third can hold.

Faction One • ~40%

The Restorationists

Believe Vijayanagara should be rebuilt as it was — the empire perfect, the throne occupied, the serpent-network active beneath every palace, the dispossessed nobility re-seated. Their argument is not hatred. It is grief. They are the people whose ancestors died when the empire fell, and they want the empire’s death to be undone.
Leadership: Bhoomi-Takshaka (architect) • Krishnadevaraya-Bhrata (her twin, until he defects) • the dispossessed Bloodline-A noble houses • mining consortium money • foreign heritage-investment funds
Faction Two • ~40%

The Reformers

Believe Hampi’s ruins are a teaching, not a wound. Sage Vidyaranya’s spirit speaks from the river: kingdoms are people, not stones. They want a federated Karnataka with the trust-network Tungabhadra built — village-mesh, shared meals, the elephant-bond as living memory rather than imperial right. They include the new artisan class, the tech-mesh operators, and most of Hampi’s working population.
Leadership: Tungabhadra • Hampi-Veera (the Stone Dancer) • Mysuru-Dhanush (the Sandalwood Archer) • Neelakantha (the Indian-Roller scout) • Krishnadevaraya-Bhrata after his defection • the Chief Engineer of New Hampi
Faction Three • ~20%

The Tribal Hadali / Lambani

Karnataka’s pre-imperial inhabitants — the Banjara-descended Lambani communities and the Hadali clan-lines that lived in the Deccan before Vijayanagara existed. The empire’s rise displaced them. The empire’s fall did not return what was taken. Tungabhadra had ignored them in Chapter One. They have the dharmic deciding vote in Chapter Two — whoever the Hadali bless wins the moral argument. They hold it back until the very end.
Leadership: Veerappa-Hadali (the elder who is crowned in the final ceremony) • the Lambani matriarch-council • the forest-edge cousins who fish the Tungabhadra above Hampi

Key Characters — This Chapter

Hero • Dies at Climax

Tungabhadra

The River Queen Who Broke Her Own Throne

The same woman from Chapter One — bronze armor, jasmine in her hair, Gajadanda mace. The change is internal: she has finally accepted that honoring the predecessors and breaking the violence-cycle cannot both be done. She must betray one. She chooses to destroy the throne-stone publicly with her own hands — and the temple-collapse Bhoomi-Takshaka has rigged kills her. The elephant-bond does not pass to ash. It walks down the ramp to her cousin.

New Hero • Inherits the Bond

Krishnadevaraya-Bhrata

The Quiet Architect • Bloodline A • The Maha-Adhipati-in-Waiting

Bhoomi-Takshaka’s twin brother, born nine minutes after her in the same dispossessed Bloodline-A household. He has spent thirty-six years as her court’s silent architect — reading the imperial blueprints, sketching restoration plans for her, never speaking. He does not love her plan. He loves what Hampi could be without it. He defects to Tungabhadra in the middle of Chapter Two with the blueprints of sublevel three of the buried temple — the chamber where the throne-stone sits. The elephant of Hampi walks to him at the chapter’s end, and the bond settles on his shoulders. His name maps to the historical Krishnadevaraya, greatest of the Vijayanagara emperors. He becomes Tungabhadra’s successor and the saga’s eventual Maha-Adhipati.

New Character • Heir Apparent

Hampi-of-the-Bronze

The Ward, the Bronze Child, the Witness

An orphan of the Chapter-1 temple-quake — the bronze of the Vittala Temple’s broken pillar was still warm when Tungabhadra pulled her from the rubble at age four. Now ten. Brown-eyed and quiet. Speaks rarely, but reads everyone in a room. The Restorationists file a legal claim in the Karnataka Heritage Court that she is “ward of the imperial line” because her grandmother carried Bloodline-A nobility. The court rules in their favor. She is taken to Bhoomi-Takshaka’s house. She returns of her own choice at the chapter’s end, walking out of the rubble holding the hand of the Third Herald. She will be Krishnadevaraya-Bhrata’s ward into Chapter Three.

New Character • Hadali Elder

Veerappa-Hadali

The Crowned-and-Uncrowned King of the Forest Edge

Eighty-three. A Lambani-descended Hadali elder from the forest-edge hamlets above Hosapete. Did not attend any Karnataka council in seventy years — refused on principle. Tungabhadra walks to his hamlet alone and asks. He comes. He is crowned at the public ceremony with jasmine and tribal cloth as the symbolic heir of the imperial throne-stone — one heartbeat before that throne-stone is destroyed. The Hadali never wanted the throne; they wanted the throne acknowledged-and-then-dissolved. He stands beside Krishnadevaraya-Bhrata at the chapter’s end as the council reconstitutes. His clan’s vote is the moral hinge of Chapter Three’s Unity Trial.

Cosmic Sign • The Third Herald

The Bronze-Dust Child

A Child Born in the Temple-Earthquake

The third of the Void Maw’s four heralds. He appears in the dust of the broken throne-stone at the chapter’s end. He is roughly five years old — the age the temple-earthquake’s aftermath would now be. He is untouched, unspeaking, and his shadow does not match his body. He cannot remember his own name. Hampi-of-the-Bronze takes his hand. Krishnadevaraya-Bhrata recognises — through the new bond — what the child is, and chooses not to kill him. The rule the saga learns in this chapter: remember the Herald, do not destroy him. He becomes a ward of the kingdom from Chapter Three onward, alongside Hampi-of-the-Bronze. His true identity will be revealed in Chapter Four — he is the Bloodline-A child “the temple-earthquake swallowed” in Chapter One, Tungabhadra’s forgotten infant cousin.

The Catalyst Villain — Re-Read

Name (Re-Emerged)
Bhoomi-Takshaka
New Title
The Restoration Architect — She Who Holds the Original Blueprints
Faction
Restorationists (~40% of Karnataka by Chapter 2 Beat 2)
The Shift From Chapter One
In Chapter One she was the “dark mirror” — the shadow-villain who shared Tungabhadra’s elephant bond and animated stone through corrupted serpent-craft. She was never destroyed. In Chapter Two she does not animate stone. She files paperwork. She does not curse Hampi. She publishes a restoration plan with diagrams. She is more dangerous in this chapter than in the last because her grievance is real and her solution is plausible.
Death
Dies at the climax inside the buried-temple sublevel three chamber, alongside Tungabhadra. The collapse she has rigged catches her too. She knows it will. She chooses to stay with the throne-stone she has worshipped for thirty-six years.
Mirror Point (Final)
Tungabhadra and Bhoomi-Takshaka die together inside the same chamber, neither of them killing the other. The same animal-bond. The same Bloodline. The same love of Hampi. Two custodianships fighting each other to a draw under the stone they shared.
