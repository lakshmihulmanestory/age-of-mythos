The Elephant-Bond — Transferred

Tungabhadra dies at the climax of this chapter inside the buried-temple chamber. Bhoomi-Takshaka dies with her. Two custodians of the same animal-bond, both Bloodline A, fall under the same rubble. The bond does not die. It does what the saga’s rules require — it walks down the ramp to the nearest living Bloodline-A kin.

From — Stage III (half-body, earned Ch 1)
Tungabhadra
Indian Elephant in ceremonial armor across her right arm and upper chest — half-body, the armor patterns moving like liquid metal. Dies at the throne-stone. The full-body mark she would have earned passes forward unworn.
→
To — Stage I (just earned, Ch 2 finale)
Krishnadevaraya-Bhrata
A single elephant-stroke flares across his right shoulder-blade the instant Gajasena touches his forehead. The bond settles. He has not yet earned levels 2, 3, or 4 — that is the work of Chapters Three and Four.

Saga-rule: a bond cannot transfer to a stranger. It walks the bloodline. Krishnadevaraya-Bhrata is Bloodline A — the dispossessed Anegondi branch — and Tungabhadra’s closest male kin in the kingdom. He earns the bond by remembrance, not by combat: at the chapter’s end he speaks his cousin’s name and his sister’s name aloud, in the same breath, in front of twenty thousand witnesses.

The New Sacred Tattoo — Krishnadevaraya-Bhrata’s Mark

The tattoo is the same animal — the Indian Elephant of Hampi — but the mark begins again. Krishnadevaraya-Bhrata starts at Stage I and must earn each level forward through the Maw War of Chapters Three and Four. The mark’s style is different from his cousin’s: where hers had Vijayanagara stone-carving vine patterns, his carries the architect’s line-drawing of the buried temple’s sublevel three — the chamber where she died.

The Throne-Stone — What It Was, What It Is Now

Before
The Imperial Throne-Stone of Vijayanagara
A single piece of green chlorite the size of an adult elephant, carved with the lion-yali emblem of the empire. Stood in the Royal Enclosure for five hundred years. Believed lost in 1565. Re-discovered intact in the buried-temple sublevel one by Tungabhadra in Chapter One. Roped off as exhibit, not used as throne. Until the Restorationists.
After
The Twelve-Piece Memorial
Destroyed at the new-moon ceremony in twelve pieces by Gajadanda struck twice in the same hour by both Tungabhadra and Krishnadevaraya-Bhrata together. The twelve pieces are removed from the Royal Enclosure and placed at the twelve corners of Karnataka — one piece per district capital — as a public commitment that no single seat will ever again carry the weight of the south.

Sacred Weapons — Inherited & Renamed

Ancient Weapon • Now Krishnadevaraya-Bhrata’s
Gajadanda — The Throne-Breaker
New Title
The Throne-Breaker — the only mace in Bharatavarsha known to have broken its own throne.
Ability (Earned in Ch 2)
The mace, having been used twice by both Bloodline-A heirs to destroy the same throne-stone, now carries the resonance of both lineages. When Krishnadevaraya-Bhrata holds it, the seismic strike of Chapter One is accompanied by the architect’s line-resonance — the mace can read the structural weakness of any building, not just the seismic frequency of stone.
Origin Update
Originally forged from meteorite-iron from the Tungabhadra river during Krishna Deva Raya’s reign. Now ceremonially “completed” by the throne-stone strike — a chlorite-dust crystal is fixed into the elephant-head pommel.
Modern Weapon • Inherited
Gaja-Ratha Mk.II — Architect’s Mode
Type
The same vibration-frequency impact staff Tungabhadra carried — now with the architect’s sub-mode active.
Ability
In addition to mapping underground structures in real time, the staff now overlays Krishnadevaraya-Bhrata’s thirty-six years of architectural drawings on every building it scans — he sees what a structure is, what it was meant to be, and what it could be remade as. The staff is the architect’s eye made visible to others.
Operator Note
The staff refused to operate for the first three days after the transfer. On the fourth day Krishnadevaraya-Bhrata sat with it in the rubble of the throne-stone and named every member of the Vijayanagara line of architects, generation by generation, going back nine hundred years. The staff activated mid-recitation.

The Restorationist Arsenal — Now Sealed

Bhoomi-Takshaka’s Visha-Danda (the serpent-tipped mace) and the serpent-network beneath Hampi are both ended with her. The serpent-network is mapped, documented, and sealed by Krishnadevaraya-Bhrata himself in the weeks after the ceremony. The blueprints are deposited with the Hadali council — not the imperial archive — so that no future emperor can ever reactivate what his sister built.

  • Visha-Danda: recovered from the rubble, ceremonially broken at the Tungabhadra river. The fragments are bound to a stone and dropped into the deepest pool.
  • The Serpent-Network: mapped, sealed at every junction with chlorite-dust mortar. The seal is renewable — the Hadali council inspects it once a year.
  • The Restoration Charter: filed in the Karnataka public archive. Not destroyed. Kept readable. The kingdom keeps its enemy’s argument on record — per the rule of the saga: remember, do not erase.
Void Maw Sign • Catalogue Entry • Karnataka
The Third Herald appears in the dust of the throne-stone at the chapter’s end. He is a five-year-old child whose shadow falls in the wrong direction. He cannot remember his name. He is the Bloodline-A infant the temple-earthquake “swallowed” in Chapter One — Tungabhadra’s forgotten cousin-son, now returned in the form a Maw-Herald takes. The kingdom does not kill him. Hampi-of-the-Bronze takes his hand. Krishnadevaraya-Bhrata kneels in front of him and promises a name. The saga-rule is established here: Heralds are remembered, not destroyed. Killing a Herald grows the Maw. Naming one converts him.
