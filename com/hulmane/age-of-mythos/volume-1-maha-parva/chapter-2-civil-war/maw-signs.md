# Void Maw Signs — Chapter 2 Catalogue

> Every kingdom's Chapter 2 story plants one sign of the Void Maw — an unnatural animal, a returning ghost, a herald with no shadow, a small impossibility. Each sign gets one entry below. By the chapter's end the signs cluster into a hawk-relay message: *"The mouth has opened. The Council must sit."*
>
> The signs are catalogued here per kingdom in writing-order, not alphabetical. Empty rows are placeholders for future sessions.

## Karnataka — The Third Herald

**Sign:** A child of roughly five years appears in the dust of the broken throne-stone at the chapter's end. He is untouched by the chlorite-dust. His clothes are clean. His hair is clean. His shadow falls in the wrong direction — slightly off-angle, as if some other light is touching him from a direction the moon is not in. He cannot remember his name. He does not speak.

**Witnessed by:** Hampi-of-the-Bronze (first), Krishnadevaraya-Bhrata (second), Veerappa-Hadali (third), the entire crowd of ~20,000 (collectively).

**Identity (revealed later):** The Bloodline-A infant the Chapter-1 temple-earthquake "swallowed" — Tungabhadra's forgotten cousin-son. Returned in the form the Void Maw gives its Heralds.

**Rule established by this sign:** Heralds are *remembered*, not destroyed. Hampi-of-the-Bronze takes his hand. Krishnadevaraya-Bhrata kneels and promises a name. The kingdom does not raise a weapon. This becomes the universal saga-rule for Heralds across all four chapters.

## Goa — The Second Herald (Vasco-without-a-ship)

**Sign:** A man walks out of the surf at Old Goa at first light the morning after Sagaradeva's storm. He is wet but not gasping — he walks as if he had stepped off a gangway, not as if he had swum ashore. His shirt is white linen and his breeches are dark wool, cut in the seventeenth century. The wool is salt-stained but not aged. The pattern of staining does not match a single immersion. His shadow points slightly off from the morning sun's direction. He cannot remember his own name. He cannot remember any ship. He looks at Mormugao Bay as if he had expected something else to be there.

**Witnessed by:** the Old Goa ruins-watchman (first), Padre Fernando Cordeiro (second), Ferreira-de-Sangam (third).

**Identity (revealed later):** A Sagaravamsha-line sailor who was meant to be the line's heir before Sagaradeva's great-grandfather. Lost at sea in 1731. *Forgotten when he returned* — and that forgetting is what the Void Maw has given a body.

**Name given:** *Vasco-without-a-ship* — by Ferreira-de-Sangam at her front step.

**Rule reinforced:** The Karnataka rule holds. Heralds are named, not killed. Padre Cordeiro recognises him from the Karnataka news two months earlier. He does not raise a weapon. He walks the man to Ferreira's house.

## Maharashtra — The Burnt Squirrel-Nest

**Sign:** An Indian Giant Squirrel's nest at the top of a Sahyadri ironwood tree is found burned. The tree below is untouched. There is no fire-line in the grass, no smoke-stain on the lower canopy, no scorched bark, no carbonised approach-path. The carbonisation is upper-canopy-only — the nest itself, and roughly a one-metre radius around it, suspended in mid-air seventeen metres above an untouched forest floor. The squirrel that lived in it is gone. No body. No feathers. No claws. No drag-marks. No predator-prints in the grass below. The ash is room-temperature when found. The carbonisation has happened from inside the nest outward, not from any external fire.

**Witnessed by:** a Lohagad fort-runner (first), Shivagati (second; climbs the tree herself to confirm), Tanaji-the-Second (third; builds the shrine).

**Mechanism (inferred through the squirrel-bond):** The Void Maw has read the Sahyadri and removed one squirrel from the record. The carbonisation is what remains when a creature's presence is erased without anything ever having been there to remove. This is *not* a Herald — there is no person, no body. This is something new.

**Rule extended:** The Karnataka rule (Heralds are named, not killed) holds for absences too. Shivagati does not retaliate. Tanaji-the-Second builds a small shrine of Sahyadri rock and packed clay at the tree's base. The shrine becomes a pilgrimage site within the season. People come from Pune and Mumbai.

**Significance (for the catalogue):** Maharashtra's Maw sign is the first *place-based* sign in the chapter. Karnataka and Goa's signs were Heralds — *people*. Maharashtra establishes the second form. Future signs in the chapter will follow this pattern: people (Heralds), places (the burnt nest), and absence-of-things (later signs across the remaining 27 kingdoms).

## Uttar Pradesh — The Clear Ganga

**Sign:** On the morning after the Bara Imambara assembly (the day Yamunadatta is crowned and Gangaputra steps down to the regent role), the Ganga at Varanasi runs glass-clear. From Assi Ghat to Manikarnika Ghat — the full length of the principal Varanasi waterfront — the river-bottom is visible from boat to bank for the entire day. The river that has carried Himalayan silt for two hundred thousand years runs without suspension for one full day. The bottom-fauna — mollusks, turtles, the occasional river-dolphin — can be seen going about its business in plain view.

**Witnessed by:** the Assi Ghat boatman pushing off at first grey light (first), the local press by 8 a.m., the national news-channels by 10 a.m., the Varanasi scientific establishment by noon, half a million tourists and pilgrims through the day.

**Mechanism (inferred through the barasingha-bond):** The Void Maw has read the Ganga and erased her silt-memory for one day. The carbonisation-of-absence pattern that began at Maharashtra's burnt squirrel-nest extends here to UP's river-silt — the Maw can take history out of a substance, and the substance becomes visible-without-memory. Pundits panic. Scientists scramble for sediment-samples and find none. The clear water is real and uncountable.

**Rule reinforced + extended:** The Karnataka rule (do not destroy, mark) holds. Gangaputra orders a small shrine built at Assi Ghat by the **boatmen-guild** (notably not by the religious order, not by the bureaucracy — by the working river-class itself). Stone and packed earth. A single carved barasingha-hoof print on the riverside face. The water returns to muddy at sunset, exactly. The shrine remains.

**Significance (for the catalogue):** UP's sign is the **first landscape-scale** Maw sign in the chapter. The catalogue is now showing a clear escalation pattern:

## Madhya Pradesh — The Motionless Herd at Kanha

**Sign:** A herd of sixty barasingha in Kanha National Park's Sonf meadow is reported standing motionless in a clearing. They do not graze. They do not move. They face east. They stand for eleven days. They are unhurt — breathing normal, eyes tracking, no dehydration. They are simply not doing what grazing animals do. On the twelfth day, late in the afternoon, one of the herd lowers her head and begins to graze. The others follow within ten minutes. **None of them remember the eleven days.** The pause-time is a blank in the herd's behavioural memory — visible through the bond as an empty interval.

**Witnessed by:** a junior Kanha forest-guard (first), the Kanha range office (second, by Tuesday), Aranya-Devi at the Court XI desk that no longer exists (third — she sends the telegram to Dvadashashringa), Dvadashashringa himself (fourth, alone in the Sonf meadow on day nine).

**Mechanism (through the bond):** The Maw lives in the gap between knowing-the-question and answering-it. The herd was caught in that gap because the human institution above them (the twelve courts) was. The herd's collective behavioural memory was wiped of the eleven days — not a removal of a creature (Maharashtra's burnt nest), not a removal of substance-memory (UP's clear Ganga), but a removal of **elapsed time** from a multi-creature collective consciousness.

**Rule extended:** The Karnataka rule holds. Dvadashashringa marks the clearing with a small sandstone-and-packed-earth shrine. A single barasingha-antler is carved in low relief on the eastern face — the direction the herd had faced. The Kanha forest guards become permanent shrine-keepers. The shrine remains.

**Significance (for the catalogue):** MP's sign is the chapter's first **multi-creature** event — sixty animals at once, eleven days of vanished behavioural-memory. The escalation pattern is now five rungs:

**Witnessed by:** Saraighat ferry-crews and news-channel boats (event 1, first); the Geological Survey of India monitoring station (event 1, second, recorded in public-archive dataset); Kaziranga park-rangers on the morning patrol-route (event 2, first); the bonded High Priestess at Kamakhya through the rhinoceros-bond (both events, simultaneously, through the bond itself).

The river forgot its direction; the herd forgot seven hours. Both forgetting-events are the Maw signing the rite's own opening.

**Rule reinforced:** The Karnataka rule holds. Kamalavarna does not retaliate. Two shrines built — one at the Saraighat reversal-point (river-bank), one at the Kaziranga kneeling-meadow (eastern edge). The ferry-crews and the park-rangers become permanent shrine-keepers.

## Meghalaya — Ka-Rhang-Wat-Snie (The Dry Day of Grief)

**Sign:** The morning after Meghanadi's funeral at the Nongriat double-decker living root bridge. Rain stops at Cherrapunji, Mawsynram, and Shillong at exactly 5:47 a.m. — the precise minute Ka-Sngi died at the bridge eleven weeks earlier. No precipitation for twenty-four hours. **Cherrapunji's rain-gauges record zero precipitation on July 30th — the only such reading in the gauges' eighty-three-year history.** The clouds are present at the usual altitude and density. They do not rain. The hill mynas in the Mawphlang sacred grove fall silent at 5:47 a.m. and remain silent for the same twenty-four hours. The locals name the day before noon: *Ka-Rhang-Wat-Snie* — the dry day of grief.

**Witnessed by:** every Khasi household with a roof and a rain-gauge (and there are many — the wettest places on earth do not lack rain-gauges); the Mawphlang sacred grove's resident priest-keepers; the India Meteorological Department's Cherrapunji station which records zero precipitation; the international monsoon-research instrument-array at the Mawsynram observatory.

**Mechanism (through the dormant clouded-leopard bond):** The Maw lives in the gap between a question and its institutional answer. Meghanadi's council speech, her settlement-draft, and her death framed the question (how does a matrilineal kingdom integrate patrilineal claimants without surrendering its line). The institutional answer — the Mixed Council, U-Khrawkmen's regent-by-naming inheritance, Ka-Sienlyngdoh's future bond-acceptance — is still forming. The dry day is the gap made meteorological. The absence of rain IS the absence of an answer.

**Rule extended:** The Karnataka rule holds. U-Khrawkmen's first formal act as inheritor is to order the day added to the state calendar as a permanent annual observance. Three district councils ratify the order in concurrent emergency sessions on the same day — first time in Meghalaya's post-1972 history. The shrine at the Mawphlang sacred grove edge is built by week's end; sandstone, packed earth, single cloud-pattern carved in low relief on the western face. The grove's elder hill myna sings the dedication-song at the opening — the first myna-song since the dry day began.

**Significance (for the catalogue):** Meghalaya's sign is the chapter's **first anti-weather** event — the *absence* of monsoon rain in monsoon territory. Six rungs of escalation became seven:

Two rows of dense phumdi-clusters inside the arc form what the pilot describes as "teeth-like." The arrangement holds for seven minutes. The mouth then closes — the two arcs touch at the centre. The closed configuration holds for ninety seconds. The phumdis drift back to their normal scatter pattern. By minute thirteen the lake is unmarked.

## Sikkim — The Red Panda at Five Thousand Four Hundred Metres (First Species-Lethal-Altitude Experiment)

Ear-tag confirms it as a known Khangchendzonga National Park individual previously recorded at three thousand eight hundred metres in 2022. Observed for **nine consecutive days** through high-power optics. It does not descend below five thousand four hundred metres.

**Witnessed by:** Tashi-Bhutia the ranger at first sighting (7:43 a.m. December 2nd); the Khangchendzonga National Park Service Gangtok central-office by 8:00 a.m.; the Sikkim state-government wildlife-affairs department by 8:30 a.m.; Pema-Chöden at the monastery ward-coordination office by 9:16 a.m. (the standing-recipient routing protocol established by the 1967 inter-departmental memorandum-of-understanding); Kanchenjunga at the inner sanctum doorway by 9:36 a.m. (concluding his morning-prayer); subsequently the nine bonded-kingdom senior guardians via Sarus-pigeon cascade-alert by sunset of the same day.

## Arunachal Pradesh — The First Herald (First Face-Erasure)

**Sign:** Six-fifty-seven a.m. on the morning of the Tawang Dawn-Shadow Temple's consecration. Roing village in the lower Dibang valley — approximately two hundred and eighty kilometres east of Tawang, at the kingdom's eastern edge. The morning's first-light reached Roing at five-oh-eight a.m. (the time-zone-staggered ridges' first-light walks east-to-west across the state across thirty-eight minutes). The village is going about its standard Tuesday morning. A man walks into the village from the east. Plain Adi-tribe homespun cotton shawl, undyed, no clan-pattern. Barefoot. Carries nothing. The sun is at an eastern angle of approximately thirty-two degrees above the horizon — the standard mid-morning angle that casts every person, animal, and post-mounted lamp a long westward shadow of about one-point-six times the casting object's height. **He casts no shadow.** The dirt-path beneath his bare feet shows no shadow-track, no foot-impression, no compression of the dirt. He walks the eight-hundred-metre length of the village's main path at a steady one-point-three metres per second pace; the walk takes eleven minutes and approximately fifteen seconds. Approximately one hundred and thirty villagers see him during the walk. Each of them sees the no-shadow. He continues out of the village at the western end and walks toward the Dibang river. None of the villagers follows.

**Within ninety minutes of his passage, none of the witnesses can remember what he looked like.** They remember he was a man. They remember the homespun. They remember the bare feet. They remember the no-shadow. They cannot remember his face. The eleven-year-old schoolboy Tashi-Dorjee at school during the third hour: *"I do not know.
