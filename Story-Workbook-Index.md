## The Volume-Level Connection Map
*When you upgrade any single story, keep these threads intact — they are the load-bearing walls of the whole novel.*

### A. The Six Regional Dominants (who each hero becomes)
- **South →** Krishnadevaraya-Bhrata (rises *behind* the Vijayanagara story; the future Maha-Adhipati). Chittaranga, Krishnaveni, Parashurama VII feed the South cluster.
- **West →** **Simhavikrama** (Gujarat lion) fused with the **Goan gaur** (from *The Fleet of the Drowned*); **Shivagati** is his witness; **Mrigatrishna** his counsellor-elder.
- **North →** **Gangaputra** (abdicates to heir **Yamunadatta**); **Kurukshetraa** becomes regent; **Panchanada/Himavati/Sharad-Pandit/Kasturika** are his cluster.
- **East →** Jagannath-Bandhu (Jagannathi's line); **Onge-Nakshatra, Sundarvani, Vajramukha, Vanajara** feed the East cluster.
- **Central →** **Dvadashashringa**; his daughter **Sutrakara** rises *above* his seat; Dandakarni's line federates under him.
- **Northeast →** **Moirangthem** (the lone dissenter); the seven NE kingdoms (Kanchenjunga/Udayagiri/Dzulevira/Meghanadi/Tlangvala/Tripurari) form his cluster.

### B. Threads that must be planted in Chapter 1 (for later payoff)
- **"What is read aloud cannot be burned"** (*The Snow Library*) = the literal cure for the **Maw** in Ch 4. This is the volume's thematic root — keep it sacred.
- **The Maha-Adhipati seed:** Krishnadevaraya-Bhrata and his villain-twin Bhoomi-Takshaka; the orphan **Hampi-of-the-Bronze**.
- **Krishnaveni's bond** to the future king — her death is named first inside the Maw (Ch4).
- **The Goan gaur migration** (*Fleet of the Drowned* → *Two Lions* / *Brother and Sister*) fuses the West.
- **Krodha** of Sikkim (plant in *The Monk…*) → a Maw-Herald who walks *back into the Maw* (Ch4).
- **Sutrakara** (plant in *The Twelve-Move War*) → the youngest at the Sanctuary; poisoned in Ch4.
- **The "Pause"** (plant in *The Last Dance of the Sangai*) → Moirangthem's dissent at the Sanctuary.
- **The Yavana-Khanda / Volume II seed** (*The Unlocking*) → Onge-Nakshatra becomes the bridge to Volume II.

### C. Recurring "naming, not killing" motif
Every Ch1 villain is a **wound, not a monster** (grief, forgetting, exploited virtue, sibling hurt). Preserve this — it is what makes the Ch4 resolution (name the dead, seal the Maw, build a council not a throne) feel earned.

### D. The external human villain network
**Aulakh-International-Lahore** (introduced in Ch2) funds the civil wars from outside. When upgrading Ch1 villains, you can *retroactively* plant its fingerprints (a forger, a suspicious financier) so Ch2 doesn't feel like it arrives from nowhere.

## How to use this workbook
1. Open any story's folder → read its **`story-deep-dive.md`**.
2. Work the **task checklist** to expand that story toward ~6,000–8,000 publishable words.
3. Before you finalise, check this index's **Connection Map** so your changes still feed the volume.
4. Each deep-dive already ends with a **Volume link** task — that's the one you must not skip.
