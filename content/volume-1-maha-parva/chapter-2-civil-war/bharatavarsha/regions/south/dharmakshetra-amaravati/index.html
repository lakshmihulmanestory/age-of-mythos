Andhra Pradesh • South Region

For thirty-four years the kingdom believed Krishnaveni was an only child. The twin had been lost at birth. The kingdom had refused to remember. Kalaniksha walked back across the Godavari delta in the second week of monsoon with three hundred peasant-fishermen behind her and a forty-seven-page family-archive document in her left hand. The twin was always known. The kingdom just chose not to know. Krishnaveni meets her at Kollur cliff at dawn. The river is waiting. The hand that holds the urumi must learn to hold the strike back.

The Hero (Survives, Stage III by Mercy-as-Discipline)

Krishnaveni — The River’s Daughter Who Did Not Kill Her Twin

Thirty-five. Born first by twenty-three minutes, by the family-archive’s newly-recovered birth-witness affidavit; raised as an only child for thirty-four years because the kingdom refused to remember the twin. The dual urumi-bearer of Godavari and Krishna; the kingdom’s state hero since the post-Narakasura confirmation in 2023. Fights Kalaniksha at the Kollur cliff at dawn on day forty-seven, loses the Krishna-urumi in the contest’s eleventh-minute disarm, holds her Godavari-urumi’s killing-stroke at the moment Kalaniksha lies disarmed at the cliff-edge. The held-strike is the Stage III moment. Per restructure §4 she dies in early Chapter Three in the twin-confrontation’s final phase; the river-bond transfers via Bloodline-A to Krishnadevaraya-Bhrata; Pulakeshi-Babu kills Kalaniksha afterwards.

The Catalyst Villain

Kalaniksha — The Spared Twin Who Returned With the Family-Archive in Her Left Hand

Thirty-five. Krishnaveni’s lost twin. Born twenty-three minutes after Krishnaveni; placed at birth with the Eluru-line midwife’s family because the family-feared bloodline-double-inheritance; raised in the delta-fishing villages as an orphan-foundling; rediscovered her true lineage at age twenty-three when she found the same family-archive document in a different branch’s storeroom. Was spared by Krishnaveni at the Ch 1 climax confrontation in 2023 when the twin-revelation first surfaced. Spent the intervening two years organising the river-bound faction across the Godavari and Krishna delta peasant-fishermen. Returns publicly on the second week of monsoon (week 6 of the chapter) with three hundred fishermen marching with her up the Krishna riverbank to Amaravati. Claims the river-throne as the elder twin (which she is not; the family-archive proves Krishnaveni was born first). Defeats Krishnaveni’s Krishna-urumi in the Kollur cliff contest. Exiled across the Tirupati border the same evening. Per restructure §6 she dies in early Chapter Three killed by Pulakeshi-Babu after she kills Krishnaveni.

The Lineage-Reveal Brother • New Throne-Liaison

Pulakeshi-Babu — The Bloodline-A Historian Who Is Krishnaveni’s Brother

Forty-three. The kingdom’s senior Bloodline-A historian; the chair of the Andhra Pradesh State Archive’s pre-independence-records cell; the author of the standard three-volume reference work The Bloodline-A Cousin Network in Southern Bharatavarsha, 1700-2020. Was thought across the kingdom to be a distant Bloodline-A historian, no closer kinship to Krishnaveni than the four-generations-back maternal cousin he had documented in volume two. Discovered in his own grandfather’s safe in week four of the chapter a forty-seven-page family-archive document — written in 1990 by his grandfather and sealed under a thirty-five-year embargo — revealing that he is Krishnaveni’s elder brother by their mother’s first marriage. The reveal triggers the kingdom-council’s emergency cousin-claim ratification with Krishnadevaraya-Bhrata’s Karnataka, establishing the Bloodline-A southern-bloc federation arrangement. Per Ch 3 plan he inherits AP’s visible throne after Krishnaveni’s death; pledges fealty to Krishnadevaraya-Bhrata’s Karnataka Dominant seat. Per Ch 4 plan he serves as the federation’s standing archive-and-lineage authority.

The Three-Way Faction Split

Dharmakshetra Amaravati has answered the river-and-delta question one way since the post-Narakasura reconstruction: Amaravati distributes, the delta receives, the upstream is restored. Chapter Two splits the answer across the body of one woman split in two.

Faction One • ~38%
The Delta-Bound • With Krishnaveni
The kingdom’s delta-rice farming communities and the post-Narakasura restored upstream villages who hold that Krishnaveni’s ten years of governance have honoured both the delta and the urbanite needs. Approximately two-point-three million constituents across the Godavari and Krishna deltas. Their argument is the kingdom’s post-Narakasura institutional reform was real; their case is documented in the kingdom’s standing water-distribution-and-rice-yield records. Krishnaveni’s base across the chapter.
Faction Two • ~32%
The River-Bound • With Kalaniksha
The delta peasant-fishermen and the river-bank Buddhist-tradition villages along the Krishna’s middle-reach who hold that the post-Narakasura reconstruction prioritised urbanite Amaravati at the expense of the river-bound villages that had suffered most during Narakasura’s drought-tyranny. Approximately one-point-nine million constituents. Their argument is the institutional reform has been incomplete; their case is documented in the unmet-petition register of the kingdom-council’s redress-and-restitution cell. Kalaniksha’s base from week six onward.
Faction Three • ~30%
Capital Amaravati • Neutral, Opportunistic
The capital city’s administrative-class, business-community, and government-services workers who hold no formal position on the twin-claim. Approximately one-point-eight million constituents. Their pattern: wait until the contest of arms is decided, then ratify whichever twin holds the river-throne the morning after. The kingdom-council’s chief-secretary (a forty-eight-year-old administrator named Lakshmi-Tholeti) maintains the capital’s neutrality formally; the central business-district’s parakeet-courier mesh-network operates without ideological prejudice. The neutrality is its own institutional position; the chapter’s pedagogical pivot is the capital’s admission on day forty-eight that the contest could not have been resolved by the capital’s neutrality alone.

The Void Maw Sign — The Delta That Lost a Metre Overnight

The morning after the Kollur cliff contest. The Krishna river’s mouth at Hamsaladeevi village in the Andhra Pradesh delta. The Bay of Bengal’s standard tide-line at the village’s eastern beach has, between sunset on the contest-day and sunrise the next morning, retreated landward by exactly one metre. The retreat is across the full village-beach’s six-hundred-metre east-west breadth. The displaced land is not flooded; the land is gone. The Bay of Bengal’s waters now lap one metre inland of where they had lapped the previous evening. There has been no storm; the night’s tide-gauge reading at the Hamsaladeevi observation-station shows a normal monsoon-tide of one-point-three metres at high-water at three forty-seven a.m.; the standard tide-cycle has retreated to two-point-one metres at low-water at nine-fifty-three a.m. The land that has been removed is not in the water and is not on any surveyor’s map; the land has been erased. The Andhra Pradesh State Survey Office’s satellite-imagery confirms the discrepancy across all three commercial satellite-tracking services. The land’s mass — approximately six hundred cubic metres of delta-soil at the village’s eastern beach — is not accounted for. The Maw has demonstrated the capacity to erase a unit of land from the kingdom’s geography. The Maw is asking: can your kingdom continue to exist when its territory is being un-measured at the rate of one metre per night? The kingdom’s answer must include the Sanctuary in Chapter Three. Per the cross-kingdom paired-sign-naming protocol established with Kerala’s black coconut, the delta-loss is recognised as the inverse of Kerala’s material-permanence — Kerala’s coconut preserves what should rot; AP’s delta erases what should remain.
