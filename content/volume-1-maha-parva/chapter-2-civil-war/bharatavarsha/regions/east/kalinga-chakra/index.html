The Three-Way Faction Split — And the Shadow Fourth

Kalinga-Chakra has answered the Jagannath-deity question one way for two thousand years: the three cults — Shabara (the original wood-bearers), Brahmin (the priestly families), and Sea-Merchant (the Bay-of-Bengal trade guilds) — have each held one third of the standing-deity-administration in rotation. Chapter Two splits the rotation along the cult-lines.

The Shabara • The Original Wood-Bearers
The Shabara tribal community of the standing-Daru-Brahma neem-wood tradition. The original carvers of the standing-deity-trio (Jagannath, Balabhadra, Subhadra) every twelve years at the Nabakalebara ceremony. Their argument: the deity-tradition began with their ancestor Vishvavasu who first found the standing-blue-stone in the standing-Nilagiri-hills; the kingdom-council’s standing-charter must restore the Shabara as the senior-cult under the standing-tradition’s standing-origin-priority. Subhadra-of-the-Wood’s base.
Faction Two • ~33%
The Brahmin • The Priestly Families
The standing 36 Brahmin priestly families of the Puri Jagannath Temple administration (the standing Pati-Mahapatra, Suara, Daitapati, and Garabadu seva-lineages). Their argument: the standing-charter has been priestly-administered since the 12th century under Chodaganga Deva’s 1135 CE temple-foundation; the priestly-administration is the kingdom-council’s standing-institutional-record-keeping arm; the Shabara have always been wood-bearers but never administrators. Jagannathi’s base (as standing engineer-priestess); Jagannath-Bandhu’s base.

The Sea-Merchant • The Bay-of-Bengal Trade Guilds

Their argument: the deity-cult is a maritime-tradition; the standing-Bay-of-Bengal trade-network is the kingdom-council’s standing-economic engine; the Sadhabas have funded the standing-temple-administration through the standing-trade-tax since 1138 CE; the kingdom-council’s amended charter must give the Sea-Merchant cult senior-administrative seat-equivalence with the priestly Brahmin.
The Shadow Fourth • Chakra-Bheda’s False Reconciliation
Chakra-Bheda walked through the city in the three-tradition syncretic costume from spring 2025 onward, claiming to be the Fourth Cult who would reconcile the three. Held private audiences with the senior delegates of each cult across approximately six months. Unmasked at Mausi Maa Temple at noon Sunday day-two by Subhadra-of-the-Wood’s public reading of the costume’s syncretic-impossibility. Killed by the assembled three-cult crowd within four minutes. The kingdom-council’s amended charter’s Sixth Clause now criminalises standing-religious-tradition-impersonation as a kingdom-council standing-offence.

The Void Maw Sign — The Konark Wheel That Turned Counterclockwise

The Konark Sun Temple at Konark, sixty-five kilometres south of Puri on the standing-Bay-of-Bengal coast. The thirteenth-century-CE Narasimhadeva-I temple is built in the form of a colossal stone chariot with twenty-four wheels, each wheel three metres in diameter, each wheel carved with standing-spoke-and-axle precision; the wheels are also functional sundials, reading the time to the standing-minute through the standing-spoke-shadow protocol. The wheels have not turned since the temple’s 1255 CE consecration. They are carved stone; they are not designed to turn; they have stood at their standing-positions for 771 years.

At twelve-oh-three a.m. on Tuesday week seven — approximately four hours after Jagannathi’s chariot completed the standing-Rath-Yatra at the Gundicha Temple’s standing-arrival-threshold — the seven southern-face wheels of the Konark Sun Temple begin to turn counterclockwise. The turning is approximately one degree per minute. The standing-archaeological-monitoring cell’s on-site night-watchman (a fifty-four-year-old administrator named D. Mohanty) observes the turning at twelve-oh-six a.m. He confirms by torch-light cross-reference against the standing-position photographs on the standing-monitoring tablet. The standing-stone is turning. There is no apparent mechanism for the turning — the stone is not on bearings, the axle is solid sandstone, the wheel-and-axle are carved from a single block.

The turning continues for thirteen hours at the standing-one-degree-per-minute rate (each wheel completes one full counterclockwise rotation across approximately three hundred and sixty minutes). All seven wheels turn through the standing-thirteen-hour window; they stop at one-oh-three p.m. Tuesday in their original standing-position. The kingdom-council’s standing-Maw-sign-recognition cell’s preliminary report at four-eleven p.m. Tuesday confirms the standing-turning as Maw-sign-categorised. The Maw has demonstrated the capacity to turn carved-stone wheels of a thirteenth-century temple counterclockwise for thirteen hours through no mechanical mechanism. Per the cross-region paired-sign-naming protocol the Konark counterclockwise-rotation pairs with Assam’s Reversed Brahmaputra — the river that flowed east-to-west for one hour during the Mahavidya rite. Both signs are reversals of standing-natural-direction. The pair is the saga’s fourth formally-recognised cross-region Maw-sign pair: direction-reversal.
