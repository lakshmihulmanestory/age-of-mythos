The Barasingha-Bond — The Cracked Stage III

Dvadashashringa earns his Stage III foreleg-mark at the moment the Dwadash staff breaks in three pieces — the bond responds to the act of dissolution by completing the mark. But the bond does not approve. The mark forms cracked down the centre: a single line of skin running through the antler-curve where the bond’s ink does not settle. The crack is permanent. It is the saga’s first marked-mark — a tattoo that records what its earning cost. He will carry it into Chapters Three and Four; the Stage IV full-body mark, when it eventually comes, will form around the crack and not over it.

The Crack • What It Records
The bond cracks because the act that earned it was a violation of the bearer’s own founding principle. Dvadashashringa founded the twelve-court system. He dissolved it himself to save the famine-dying. The bond does not punish him for the dissolution — the dissolution was dharmically correct — but the bond records the price. Future bearers reading the catalogue will recognise the pattern: when a hero must betray their own first stake to do the right thing, the bond’s mark carries the betrayal as a visible scar. This is distinct from the Karnataka throne-stone destruction mark (which transferred at death) and the Maharashtra double-bond mark (which arrived in addition). MP’s mark is the first internally-modified mark in the saga.

The Dwadash Staff — In Three Pieces

Dvadashashringa’s ceremonial weapon — a six-foot twelve-pronged ironwood staff with twelve solar-gold prong-tips — breaks during the court-dissolution. He swings it twelve times. It cracks on the eighth strike, splits on the eleventh, ends in three pieces by the twelfth. He does not have it repaired. He carries the three pieces wrapped in saffron cloth into every subsequent assembly.

Before the Dissolution
The Whole Dwadash
Six feet of central Indian ironwood, twelve prongs in solar-gold, ceremonial weight only. Used as a procedural-opening tool at every assembly: the king strikes the floor once to open session, once to close. Cared for by a single master craftsman of the Bhopal court since 2003.
After the Dissolution
The Three-Piece Saffron Bundle
Three pieces. The upper third with five prong-tips. The middle third with four prong-tips. The lower third with three prong-tips. Wrapped in saffron cloth in their broken arrangement. Carried into every Council session into Chapters Three and Four. The bundle becomes the saga’s loudest reminder that late action is paid in bodies.

The twelve solar-gold prong-tips that scattered across the assembly floor during the dissolution are collected by Sutrakara that evening. She keeps them in a small box on her writing-desk. She has, by Chapter Four, given eleven of them away — one to each of the eleven chief justices, as a private remembering-gift. She keeps the twelfth for herself.

Sacred Weapons — Pre- and Post-Dissolution

Ancient Weapon • Broken
Dwadash — The Twelve-Pronged Staff
Status
Broken in three pieces during the court-dissolution. Not repaired. Carried in saffron cloth.
Symbolism (Now)
The staff broke at the eighth, eleventh, and twelfth strikes — the breaks correspond to the moments at which the act of dissolution crossed the threshold of irreversibility. The break-pattern is read by later scholars as a dharmic-correctness signal: the bond signed off on the dissolution by allowing the staff to break exactly at its acts of completion.
Modern Weapon • New This Chapter
Bhopal-Tantra — The Emergency Court Network
Type
A single-court administration network replacing the twelve-court system. Aranya-Devi runs it operationally. Sutrakara’s arithmetic department keeps the running death-curve and decision-cycle metrics.
Ability
Resolves disaster-relief cases in days rather than weeks. Federates with UP’s Sangam-Tantra (Vidura-Babu’s network) on the third day of operation. Two Awadhi administrators sent by Vidura-Babu become the network’s permanent liaison-officers.

The Saga-Rule This Chapter Established

Saga-Rule • The Internally-Modified Mark
A bond may complete a Stage mark with a permanent internal modification if and only if: (1) the bearer earned the mark through an act that was dharmically correct AND violated the bearer’s own founding principle, (2) the violation was acknowledged publicly at the moment of the earning, (3) the bearer accepts the cost as part of the mark rather than seeking to obscure it. The cracked-mark mechanic is distinct from death-transfer (Karnataka), bond-transit (Goa), double-bond (Maharashtra), and split-bond (UP). It is the saga’s fifth bond-mechanic, and the only one in which the cost of the choice is inscribed on the bearer’s body forever.
Void Maw Sign • Catalogue Entry • Madhya Pradesh
The Motionless Herd. Sixty barasingha in Kanha National Park stand motionless in a clearing for eleven days, facing east, not grazing, not in distress. On the twelfth day they begin grazing again. None of them remember the eleven days. Mechanism (read through the bond): the herd was caught in the gap between knowing-the-question and answering-it. The Maw lives in deliberation that does not end. The herd’s collective behavioural memory was wiped of the eleven days — not a removal of a creature (Maharashtra’s burnt nest) or a removal of substance-memory (UP’s clear Ganga), but a removal of elapsed time. The Karnataka rule holds. Dvadashashringa marks the clearing with a small sandstone shrine; Kanha forest guards become its keepers. Significance (for the catalogue): MP’s sign is the chapter’s first multi-creature event — sixty animals at once, eleven days of vanished behavioural-memory. The escalation continues:
Person (Karnataka 3rd Herald, Goa 2nd Herald) → single place (Maharashtra burnt nest) → landscape (UP clear Ganga) → multi-creature (MP motionless herd) → next signs climb further still.
