The Gaur-Bond — In Transit

Sagaradeva dies releasing the cyclone he has held without his tridents. Maha-Vega has already fallen. The Gaur of Goa — the largest wild bovine in Bharatavarsha, Sagaradeva’s sacred bond — turns from the coast and walks inland through the Konkan night-forest. She is not transferring instantly. She is looking. She is the only animal-bond in the saga whose transit between heirs is dramatised in real time.

From — Stage III (half-body, earned Ch 1)
Sagaradeva
Indian gaur in full charge across his left shoulder and chest — half-body, the horns curving up his neck. Dies in the cyclone. The full-body mark he would have earned passes forward unworn.
→
In transit — arrives Ch 2 Maharashtra arc
Shivagati (Maharashtra)
Will receive the gaur-bond as a second bond on top of her existing Indian Giant Squirrel bond — the first known double-bonded hero in the saga. The gaur’s tattoo will form mid-back as a second mark beside the squirrel-tail on her shoulder.

Saga-rule (refined this chapter): A bond walks. It does not teleport. The gaur of Goa is bonded to Sagaradeva by birth and to Bloodline E by lineage; Shivagati is the nearest Bloodline-E heir who can receive a sea-and-stone bond. Per restructure D9, this double-bond will be further transferred to Simhavikrama of Gujarat in Chapter Three when Shivagati falls. The gaur walks twice in Volume I before she stops.

The Tridents — Samudra and Lahara, Lost

Sagaradeva throws both tridents at Maha-Vega in the cyclone. Samudra strikes at the storm-form’s shoulder-joint and opens it. Lahara strikes at the man-inside-the-storm’s throat and ends him. Both tridents go down into Mormugao Bay with Maha-Vega’s body. The Old Fishermen recover the body. They do not recover the tridents. The bay is fifty-three metres deep at that point. The currents are strong. The tridents are gone.

Samudra (Ancient)
The Wave-Edge
Status
Lost in Mormugao Bay, ~53 m depth, at the spot where Maha-Vega’s storm-form unravelled. The Old Fishermen attempted recovery for three days and stopped. The trident is considered given to the bay, not lost.
Ability (Now Latent)
The wave-edge channelled the seaward half of the cyclone — the surge, the rip-current, the undertow. Without its mate it cannot be wielded by a living hand; if recovered, it must be paired with a new partner-trident of identical Sagaravamsha bronze.
Lahara (Ancient)
The Crest-Edge
Status
Also lost. Sank near Samudra. The Old Fishermen marked the spot with a buoy that broke loose in the same season’s natural monsoon.
Ability (Now Latent)
The crest-edge channelled the skyward half of the cyclone — the wind, the lightning, the spinning column above the storm-eye. Like its mate, it requires a partner. The two tridents together were the Sagaravamsha’s cyclone-channel. Apart, they are tridents.

A new Bloodline-E heir who can pair the tridents — were they ever recovered — would need to come from both coastal and stone-side branches of the line. Onge-Nakshatra of Sagara-Ratna (Andaman) is a candidate. Simhavikrama of Gujarat is another. The recovery itself is a Chapter Three story-seed, not a Chapter Two event.

The Tattoo Sequence — What Was Earned, What Walks

The gaur-tattoo is not transferring to a single inheritor at the end of this chapter. It is in motion. Stage IV is unearned and unassigned. The mark literally walks — on the back of a gaur in the night-forest.

Stage I • Earned Ch 1The Hoofprint
Sagaradeva’s first mark, on the left shoulder. The gaur’s cloven hoofprint with the Konkan coast outlined inside it.
Stage II • Earned Ch 1The Charging Foreleg
Down the arm. Earned when Sagaradeva defeated Maha-Vega in the spring monsoon of Chapter One.
Stage III • Earned Ch 1 FinaleHalf-Body
Across the chest. Earned during the same monsoon when Sagaradeva kept the foreign fleet (then unnamed) clear of the bay. The mark Sagaradeva carried into Chapter Two.
Stage IV • WalkingThe Full Charge — In Transit
Unearned by Sagaradeva. Not yet inscribed on Shivagati. Currently a mark walking on the gaur herself through the Konkan forest — the only stage in the saga that exists as an animal in motion rather than as ink on skin. Will inscribe on Shivagati when the gaur arrives in the Sahyadri.

Modern Weapons — What Goes to Ferreira’s House

Modern • Ancestral Communications
Sagara-Tantra Mk.I — Tide Network
What It Is
The encrypted mesh-network Sagaradeva built to coordinate the Tendulkar-line fishing villages — tide-readings, weather, boat-locations, market prices. Survived the storm because it was distributed.
New Custodian
Sona Tendulkar (Bhau’s daughter) holds the operating role; Ferreira-de-Sangam holds the strategic role. The network becomes the spine of the Goan resistance into Chapter Three.
Modern • Surveillance Counter-Net
Mauja-Drishti Mk.II — Wave-Sight Drones
What It Is
A small fleet of solar-skiff drones that patrol the Konkan coast at the new boundary line — built by the Old Fishermen with funding routed through a Pernem cooperative. Track foreign fleet movements without violating any law.
New Custodian
Padre Cordeiro inherits operational oversight after he resigns from the port consortium. The drones do not stop the consortium — they make the consortium’s every movement public.
Void Maw Sign • Catalogue Entry • Goa
The Second Herald — Vasco-without-a-ship — walks out of the surf at Old Goa at first light the morning after the storm. He wears seventeenth-century sailor’s clothing that is salt-stained but not aged. His shadow does not match the morning sun’s direction. He cannot remember his name, his ship, or the sea. Ferreira-de-Sangam takes him in. The Karnataka rule holds: Heralds are named, not killed. His true identity will be revealed in Chapter Four — a Sagaravamsha-line sailor lost at sea in 1731 and forgotten when he returned, the forgetting itself being what the Void Maw has given a body.
