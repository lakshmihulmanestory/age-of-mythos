Saga First • This Chapter
Shivagati becomes the first double-bonded hero in the saga — the Indian Giant Squirrel of the Sahyadri and the Indian Gaur of Goa. The gaur arrives three nights after Sagaradeva’s death, walking up from the Konkan forest with the black-crested bulbul above her. Half the kingdom calls it blessing. Half calls it curse. Shivagati survives Ch 2 with the double-bond settled — but her brother Vanaraksha is not finished, and the gaur came to her for a reason that has not yet been explained.

Shivagati at the moment the gaur kneels — squirrel on her shoulder, hoofprint forming on her back

The Hero (Survives Ch 2)

Shivagati — The Wall-Climber Queen

Thirty-one. Mountain-fighter. Carries Vanara (the staff her grandfather forged from a single piece of Sahyadri rock-iron). Earns her Stage III mark by climbing a fort wall and asking its commander to choose her on character, not blood.

The Catalyst Villain

Vanaraksha — The Elder Brother With the Old-Law

Her elder brother. Files a formal primogeniture claim in the Maharashtra Heritage Court. Technically correct under the old Bloodline-E succession law. Does not raise an army. Hires lawyers. Twenty-four of the forty-seven fort-commanders find the paperwork persuasive enough to swear nominal allegiance to him.

The First Voluntary Follower

Tanaji-the-Second — The Silent Veteran

Fifty. Fort-commander of the silent garrison nobody had counted in the arithmetic. Believes in neither old-law nor new-blessing. Kneels for Shivagati after she climbs his fort wall personally and presents herself unarmed at his command-room door. The first follower in the saga chosen by skill alone.

The Three-Way Faction Split — The Forty-Seven Forts

Maharashtra’s Sahyadri ridge has 47 hill-forts in active use. Most are crumbling. Most are small. All of them are in someone’s line of sight. They have been the Sahyadri’s parliament for three hundred years — not Mumbai, not Pune, the forts. Chapter Two is the season they fail to agree.

Forts that follow Vanaraksha because the old-law is comforting. Many do not love him. They love the predictability of primogeniture. Includes the coastal fort that defects mid-chapter and seizes Shivagati’s elder cousin’s grave.
Independents
The Watchers
~3-5 forts moving constantly
Forts that refuse to choose between the two siblings and declare their own command. Most return to the federation by chapter’s end — gravity pulls them back — but two stay out as permanent dissidents into Chapter Three.

The Void Maw Sign

An Indian Giant Squirrel’s nest at the top of a Sahyadri tree is found burned. The tree below is untouched. There is no fire-line, no scorched grass, no smoke-stain on the bark. The nest itself is ash. The squirrel that lived in it is gone — no body, no feathers, no claws. Only the upper canopy carbonised, and the open air below it untouched. Shivagati reads what the sign means through her squirrel-bond: the Void Maw has taken one of her squirrels. She does not raise a weapon. She marks the spot. Tanaji-the-Second builds a small shrine. The shrine becomes a pilgrimage site by the end of the season.
