Kanyakumari. The first Monday of February. Four-twelve a.m. The southernmost residence of the Sangam-tradition's oldest active scholar — the Acharya Bharati's small palm-thatched house at the Vivekananda Rock Memorial's western shore. The Acharya is eighty-three. He is Sage Agastya's eleventh-direct-apprentice; the kingdom-council's standing Sangam-tradition senior-mentor; the chair of the Madurai Sangam Hall's standing-poetry sub-committee for nineteen years; Kurinjiselvi's personal mentor since her seventeenth year.

He has been awake since three-forty-five. He has just finished his pre-dawn cup of black tea (the second steeping; the first is too strong this morning). He is wearing the standard Sangam-elder cotton, off-white, with the kingdom-council's senior-mentor blue-bordered shawl across his shoulders.

A rice-paper envelope has been pushed under his front door between four-oh-three and four-eleven a.m. He sees it at four-twelve when he stands to take the empty tea-cup to the kitchen. The envelope is approximately fifteen centimetres by ten, sealed with the standard Sangam Hall authorisation-clerk office's wax-seal in green wax (the standard rota-removal communication-protocol). The seal's impression is the authorisation-clerk Palinisi's personal cipher.

He picks up the envelope. He sits at the kitchen-table. He opens the seal with the standard Sangam-elder's thumbnail-break. He reads the rice-paper sheet inside. The sheet is single-spaced in formal Sangam-administrative Tamil. He reads it three times across the next eleven minutes.

The sheet reads, in plain Tamil: "Respected Acharya. Following the authorisation-clerk office's standing protocol for the periodic refreshment of the Sangam Hall standing-poetry sub-committee's chair, the office has determined that the Acharya's name shall be removed from the council-rota effective the next Sangam session on Thursday. The removal is administered under the standing pre-1972 charter's thirty-second clause's rotation-and-refreshment provision. The replacement-chair has not yet been confirmed; the office will communicate the replacement at the appropriate time. The Acharya's status as standing senior-mentor of the kingdom-council's Sangam-tradition remains unchanged. Authorisation-clerk Palinisi. Madurai Sangam Hall. February 1st."

The Acharya finishes the third reading at four-twenty-three. He folds the rice-paper sheet along its standard quarter-fold. He places the folded sheet inside the kitchen-table's standing-correspondence drawer.

He stands. He walks to the small writing-desk against the kitchen's eastern wall. He sits at the desk. He picks up the standing-correspondence parakeet-courier's pre-folded leaf-letter pack. He writes a single leaf-letter in his standard Sangam-elder's Tamil-Brahmi calligraphy. The letter takes him forty-three minutes. He finishes at five-oh-six a.m.

The letter is addressed to Kurinjiselvi at her Nilgiri Mountains residence. The leaf-letter is folded according to the standard parakeet-courier-fold and sealed with his personal cipher.

He walks to the front door at five-eleven a.m. He calls the courier-parakeet at the eastern lattice. The parakeet (his standing courier, a fourteen-year-old female rose-ringed parakeet named Pavalam) arrives within ninety seconds. He places the leaf-letter in her sleeve-pouch. He releases her westward at five-fourteen a.m. She flies at the standard rose-ringed-parakeet courier-pace (approximately forty kilometres per hour). She will be at the Nilgiri residence by sundown.

The Acharya returns to the kitchen. He boils a third tea. He sits at the kitchen-table. He waits.

He dies at five-forty-eight a.m. on the morning of Thursday, the fourth of February — three days after the rota-removal letter. Cause of death recorded on the kingdom's death-certificate: 'natural causes; cardiac arrhythmia consistent with the patient's age and medical history.' The Kanyakumari District Medical Officer's on-site assessment notes that the Acharya's pre-Thursday-tea cup was still on the kitchen-table and approximately one-third full when the body was found. The tea was the second steeping. The second is honest.

Kurinjiselvi is at the Nilgiri summit when Pavalam reaches her residence at five-forty-three p.m. on the chapter's opening Monday. She is sitting cross-legged on a flat granite outcrop at two thousand four hundred metres, watching the Kurinji-flower fields. The flowers have not bloomed. The bloom-year is twelve months away. The fields are the standard year-eleven grey-green configuration.

The fourteen Nilgiri tahrs of the standing-mountain herd are with her. They have been with her every dawn-and-dusk vigil for the past nine years; she has fed them daily by hand; they know her by scent and breath.

She descends the western trail at six p.m. when the residence-keeper Iyer-Mama (her standing administrative aide; a sixty-eight-year-old former Sangam-poet who took the residence-keeper seat after his retirement from the assembly in 2019) calls her back from the summit. Pavalam is at the residence's eastern lattice. The leaf-letter is in the sleeve-pouch.

Kurinjiselvi reads the letter at the residence's evening-tea room. Iyer-Mama is across the table from her.

The letter reads: "Daughter. The authorisation-clerk has removed me from the council-rota effective Thursday. The removal is the ninety-third in eighteen months. The pattern is no longer compatible with the standing pre-1972 charter's thirty-second clause's honest interpretation. The pattern is procedural-control under a different name. I will not contest the removal directly — the contest's outcome would be procedural and slow and would obscure the pattern. I will instead permit the removal's public-record-entry. The entry will be the kingdom-council's standing evidence of the pattern's scope.

The next sequence will not be mine to direct. I am eighty-three. The next forty-eight hours will likely be my last. I write to you because the next sequence will be yours. The Sangam-tradition cannot be repaired by procedural reform alone; the procedure has been the tradition for two millennia and the procedure has now been weaponised. The repair must be a public-speech that breaks the procedure's authorisation-rule. The repair must be unauthorised.

The Kurinji-flower will not bloom for another year. Your power is at the standard year-eleven attenuated state. The unauthorised speech must still take place. The eleven syllables of a Thirukkural couplet's opening will be enough. The mark will form in the chamber. The kingdom-council will be in attendance. The amendment will follow within twelve days.

The child Avvaiyar-the-Younger is twelve. She has been at the Sangam Hall every council session for the past five years under her grandmother's mentor-seat. She has memorised every silenced voice. She is the kingdom's standing living-archive without yet knowing it. Find her after the unauthorised speech. The post-amendment open-assembly evening will be hers to administer.

The poet-warrior Tholkappiyan-the-Younger is twenty-seven. He has been training at the Sangam Hall's standing poet-warrior gymnasium since age fourteen. He is the kingdom-council's standing succession-candidate for the Sangam-tradition's visible seat. Introduce him at the post-amendment open-assembly evening. The Sanctuary in Chapter Three will receive him as Tamil Nadu's formal representative. You will step back to the elder seat by the autumn.

The Madurai Manimekalai manuscript will record what has happened. The recording will not be by anyone's hand. The recording is the kingdom's evidence that the kingdom remembers.

Even the sharp sword bends before the sharper thought. The sharper thought has been the procedure for two thousand years. The thought is now the sword. The bending must be ours.

Acharya Bharati. Eleventh-direct-apprentice of Sage Agastya. Kanyakumari. February 1st."

Kurinjiselvi reads the letter twice. She does not weep. She has not wept since she was seventeen when her teacher of the Thirukkural-recitation tradition died at the same Kanyakumari residence. She does not weep now.

She says, in plain Tamil, in the Thirukkural-couplet register she uses for formal statements: "Iyer-Mama. The Acharya will be dead by Thursday. The kingdom-council will not contest the cause of death. The pattern will continue under Palinisi's authorisation-clerk office until the assembly itself rises. The assembly will not rise without a public-speech. The speech must be mine. I will walk down from the mountains on the morning of the Thursday. I will be at the Madurai Sangam Hall by the Friday morning council session. The unauthorised speech will be at the Friday session if Palinisi's authorisation-clerk office has not approved my rota-entry by then. The authorisation-clerk office will not approve. The speech will be unauthorised."

Iyer-Mama nods. He says: "The Kurinji-staff. The tahrs. The two-week walk to Madurai. The crescent-blade must be sharpened. The Thirukkural-couplet must be memorised in its full version even though only the eleven syllables will be spoken. The memorisation is the kingdom-council's standing pre-speech-preparation protocol's required completeness."

She says: "I will memorise the full couplet. The full couplet is the Acharya's own composition from 1989. The full version is one hundred and seven syllables across the standard Thirukkural seven-and-four-meter. The eleven syllables that will be spoken are the opening line's first phrase. The full version is the unsaid kingdom-council's permanent record. The eleven syllables are the speech."

She stands. She walks to the residence's western training-courtyard. She begins the standard Sangam-warrior pre-speech weapons-preparation sequence. The crescent-bladed Kurinji-staff is at her standing-stand against the western wall. The blade is sharpened across the next two hours. The tahrs watch from the southern fence.

She begins the walk down from the Nilgiris the next morning at four a.m. on Tuesday the second of February. The walk to Madurai is approximately fourteen days at the standard mountain-warrior's pace. She will be at the Madurai Meenakshi temple-city by the morning of the Friday the fifteenth of February. She will be at the Sangam Hall by ten a.m. Friday morning.

Avvaiyar-the-Younger's grandmother — a sixty-eight-year-old Sangam-tradition standing-mentor named Avvaiyar-Periya (the elder Avvaiyar; the child Avvaiyar is named after her in the standard Sangam-tradition rename-of-the-poet-child protocol) — has been attending every Sangam council session for fifty-seven consecutive years. The amendment's draft is presented at the end of day eleven. The amendment is twenty-three pages. The amendment's seven core provisions are:

First. The authorisation-clerk office is dissolved as a procedural-control institution. The office's standing pre-clearance window's 48-hour-prior submission-protocol is replaced by an open-registration protocol; any Sangam-poet-roll-registered poet may speak at any council session by signing the standing open-registration roll at the session's opening.

Second. The kingdom-council's standing public-objection-protocol's 24-hour-clarification-query window is amended to a one-hour-clarification-query window with mandatory resolution within twenty-four hours; the kingdom-council's standing-procedural-review cell is the standing-arbiter.

Third. The kingdom-council's standing-rota-removal protocol is amended to require: (a) seventy-five per cent senior-mentor seat-vote; (b) the affected poet's response-right within seven days; (c) the kingdom-council's standing-procedural-review cell's independent assessment within fourteen days.

Fourth. The kingdom-council's standing-poet-children's gallery is granted observer-and-recitation rights at the council's standing open-assembly evenings. The recitation is administered through the children's mentor-seats but the child-poets' voices are themselves the recitation.

Fifth. The Sangam-tradition's standing living-archive seat is established. The seat is non-rotational and is held by the kingdom-council's standing-recognised living-archive recogniser (the seat-holder is determined by the senior-mentor seat-vote upon evidence of qualifying memorial-capacity). The seat-holder's standing-duty is to attend every council session and to be the kingdom-council's standing-recourse for unrecorded silencings, retrieved voices, and reconstructed-from-memory un-written poems.

Sixth. The kingdom-council's standing post-amendment open-assembly evening will be held at the Sangam Hall on the evening of the twelfth-day. The evening will be open to all registered Sangam-poet-roll members and to the standing-poet-children's gallery. The standing living-archive seat's candidate-recognition-recitation will be held at the evening's opening hour.

Seventh. Palinisi's authorisation-clerk seat is dissolved. Her standing-employment within the kingdom-council is preserved through transfer to the Sangam-archives wing's junior-archivist seat under standing-mentor supervision. The transfer is not a punishment; the transfer is the kingdom-council's acknowledgement that her administrative-capacity is real and should be repurposed to the archive's standing-work. She accepts the transfer at the kingdom-council's day-twelve administrative-session.
